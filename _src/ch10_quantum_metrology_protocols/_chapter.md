goal: Compute quantum Fisher information and simulate metrology protocols from preparation to estimation
---
Quantum metrology asks how precisely a parameter imprinted on a quantum state can be estimated. The chapter starts
from estimation theory, the Cramér–Rao bound and the quantum Fisher information, computes the latter for pure and
mixed states (through the symmetric logarithmic derivative), and then simulates complete protocols: Ramsey
interferometry at the standard quantum limit, GHZ interferometry at the Heisenberg limit, and spin squeezing and
cat-state generation by one-axis twisting.

The later notebooks study what happens to metrological usefulness under scrambling dynamics, particle loss and
noise, how the quantum Fisher information can be measured with classical shadows, and how it spreads in quenched
spin chains.
