1. Introduction

Matrix product states have been trained on images, as classifiers and as generative models. Here the data come from quantum many-body states: the bit strings that a quantum simulator returns when every qubit of a prepared state is measured. Such measurement records are what experiments with trapped ions, Rydberg atoms or superconducting qubits produce, and a matrix product state (MPS) suits them for two reasons: ground states of one-dimensional Hamiltonians carry little entanglement, which grows only logarithmically with \(N\) at a critical point, and are compressed efficiently into an MPS, and an MPS can be sampled exactly and evaluated on any bit string at a cost linear in the number of qubits. The notebook assumes the matrix product states and DMRG of notebook 18, matrix product states, DMRG and TEBD and the two machine-learning models of notebook 56, tensor networks as machine-learning models and notebook 57, the MPS Born machine.

1.1 Snapshots

Let \(\vert\psi\rangle\) be a state of \(N\) qubits. Measuring every qubit in the computational basis (the common eigenbasis of all \(Z_j\)) returns a bit string \(\mathbf s=(s_0,\dots,s_{N-1})\), \(s_j\in\{0,1\}\), with the probability given by the Born rule,

\[p(\mathbf s)=\vert\langle\mathbf s\vert\psi\rangle\vert^2=\vert\psi(\mathbf s)\vert^2, \tag{1}\]

and destroys the state. One such string is a snapshot in the \(Z\) basis. Rotating every qubit by the Hadamard gate \(U_{\rm H}=(X+Z)/\sqrt2\) before the measurement gives a snapshot in the \(X\) basis, with probabilities \(\vert\langle\mathbf s\vert U_{\rm H}^{\otimes N}\vert\psi\rangle\vert^2\). The outcome \(s_j=0\) means the eigenvalue \(+1\) of \(Z_j\) (or of \(X_j\)), \(s_j=1\) the eigenvalue \(-1\), so the measured value is \((-1)^{s_j}\). A data set is a list of \(M\) snapshots of \(M\) identically prepared copies. Sites, bits and bonds are counted from 0, and bond \(j\) is the cut between sites \(j-1\) and \(j\).

1.2 Two tasks: reconstructing a state and learning a property

Part A (Section 4) is quantum state tomography with a prior. Full tomography of a density matrix of \(N\) qubits has \(4^N-1\) real parameters and needs measurements in \(3^N\) settings of single-qubit Pauli bases, as derived in notebook 23, quantum state tomography; this is out of reach beyond about ten qubits. Here the state of \(N=32\) qubits is reconstructed inside a much smaller set fixed in advance, the prior: the state is pure, its amplitudes in the \(Z\) basis are non-negative, and it is well approximated by an MPS of small bond dimension. Under the second assumption Eq. (1) can be inverted, \(\psi(\mathbf s)=\sqrt{p(\mathbf s)}\), so the state is fixed by its distribution in a single basis, and learning that distribution from snapshots reconstructs the state. The learner is the MPS Born machine, introduced in [1]. Ground states of stoquastic Hamiltonians, such as the transverse-field Ising chain used below, satisfy its sign and purity parts exactly (Section 3.1). Neural-network quantum state tomography, introduced in [2], used the same prior for ground states of the transverse-field Ising and XXZ models, which it reconstructed from \(\sigma^z\) snapshots alone with a restricted Boltzmann machine. The prior buys a single measurement basis instead of \(3^N\) settings and a model of \(O(N\chi^2)\) numbers. It costs the ability to see signs, phases or mixedness (Section 4.9); the MPS tomography of [3], which removes the sign prior by measuring neighbouring blocks of qubits in all Pauli bases, is outlined in Section 4.10.

Part B (Section 5) is property learning. An MPS classifier, introduced in [4], is trained on snapshots labelled by the phase of the state they come from and then asked, at every field, which phase the snapshots look like. Nothing is reconstructed: the classifier learns one binary property of the state from its measurement records. Training a classifier on configurations labelled by their phase and reading the transition off the point where its output changes was introduced in [5], for classical Monte Carlo configurations; here it is applied to quantum snapshots in two measurement bases. Section 5 also shows what such a crossing converges to as \(N\) grows, and which crossings converge to the critical point.

1.3 Road map

  • Section 3 defines the transverse-field Ising chain, shows that its ground state can be taken with non-negative amplitudes in the \(Z\) basis and in the \(X\) basis, with the non-degeneracy this needs checked by printed gaps, computes ground states for \(N=16\), \(32\) and \(64\) by DMRG, removes the symmetry breaking of DMRG by a parity projection, and samples snapshots in both bases. Everything is checked against exact diagonalisation at \(N=12\), against the exact free-fermion energies, and at \(N=16\) one to one against exact state vectors.
  • Section 4 reconstructs the ground state of \(N=32\) spins from \(Z\) snapshots: the estimator, the Kullback–Leibler divergence and the fidelity with their statistical errors, observables of the reconstruction from local estimators, the dependence on the number of snapshots and on the bond dimension, the field dependence across the critical point, an exact state-vector check at \(N=16\), the failure for a state with signs and for a mixed state, and the step to general MPS tomography.
  • Section 5 classifies snapshots by phase in the \(Z\) and the \(X\) basis for \(N=16\), \(32\) and \(64\), identifies which statistic of the snapshots each classifier tracks, computes the exact limits of these crossings from the free-fermion solution, locates the critical point with crossings of two system sizes, and repeats the \(N=16\) analysis with exact state vectors.
  • Sections 6–7 give the cost, the point where exact state vectors stop, and the limits of the methods.

What you will learn

Physics

  • why ground states of stoquastic Hamiltonians are sign-free, and why for them one measurement basis determines the state;
  • what \(Z\) and \(X\) snapshots of the Ising chain can and cannot reveal: the order parameter, the transverse magnetisation, the disorder operator of the Kramers–Wannier duality, signs and coherence;
  • why the field at which a classifier, or any order parameter, crosses a fixed level does not converge to the critical point, and why the crossing of two system sizes does.

Numerical methods

  • quantum state tomography with a prior, the fidelity of a reconstruction, the bound \(F\geq e^{-D_{\rm KL}}\), and statistical errors of Monte Carlo estimates from exact samples;
  • local estimators of off-diagonal observables for a positive wave function, and their zero variance for an eigenstate;
  • model selection on a validation set; exact correlation functions of the chain from Majorana determinants; scale-invariant crossings and the Binder cumulant.

Implementation practice

  • DMRG ground states as data generators, with warm starts along a field scan; a parity projection as an exact MPS operation; exact sampling in a rotated basis by a gate on the physical legs;
  • reuse of compiled training functions across data sets of one shape; validation of every estimator on a chain small enough to enumerate or against an exact solution;
  • one-to-one checks of every MPS result against exact state vectors at \(N=16\), with Lanczos in the parity sectors and snapshots drawn directly from the state vector.

# All of them are collected, with their mathematics, in the notebook
# 'Building the quantum simulator engine'.
# States are rank-N tensors of shape (2,)*N; every operator acts through ONE einsum.
# ==============================================================================
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Elementary matrices: Paulis, Clifford generators, rotations
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
I2 = jnp.eye(2, dtype=CDTYPE)
X = jnp.array([[0, 1], [1, 0]], dtype=CDTYPE)
Y = jnp.array([[0, -1j], [1j, 0]], dtype=CDTYPE)
Z = jnp.array([[1, 0], [0, -1]], dtype=CDTYPE)
S = jnp.array([[1, 0], [0, 1j]], dtype=CDTYPE)                      # phase gate, S^2 = Z

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Time evolution: TEBD / Trotter-Suzuki, Chebyshev, Krylov, exact
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
def lanczos(matvec, v0, m=60, reorth=True):
    """m-step Lanczos iteration: orthonormal basis V of the Krylov space span{v, Hv, H^2 v, ...}
    in which H is TRIDIAGONAL:   T = V^dag H V = tridiag(beta, alpha, beta).

    Recurrence     w = H v_j - alpha_j v_j - beta_{j-1} v_{j-1},   alpha_j = <v_j|H|v_j>,   beta_j = ||w||.
    `reorth=True`  re-orthogonalises against all previous vectors (cures the loss of orthogonality of
                   floating-point Lanczos at the price of O(m^2) inner products).
    Only `matvec` (H|psi>) is needed -> matrix-free.  Returns (alphas, betas, V[m, 2,..,2]).
    """
    v = v0 / jnp.linalg.norm(v0)
    V, alphas, betas = [v], [], []
    w = matvec(v)
    for j in range(m):
        a = jnp.real(jnp.vdot(V[j], w))
        alphas.append(a)
        w = w - a * V[j] - (betas[-1] * V[j - 1] if j > 0 else 0.0)
        if reorth:
            for u in V:
                w = w - jnp.vdot(u, w) * u
        b = jnp.linalg.norm(w)
        if j == m - 1 or float(b) < 1e-12:
            break
        betas.append(b)
        V.append(w / b)
        w = matvec(V[-1])
    return np.array(alphas, dtype=float), np.array(betas, dtype=float), jnp.stack(V)

# Variational circuits: ansatz, gradients, optimizers
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
_SPIN_MPS = {"0": [1, 0], "1": [0, 1], "+": [1, 1], "-": [1, -1]}      # same characters as product_state
LAM_CUT = 100 * float(jnp.finfo(RDTYPE).eps)                           # Schmidt values below this are padding
@jax.jit
def mps_to_state(B):
    """Padded MPS -> state tensor (validation only, O(2^N)).  The dummy boundary bonds are index 0.
    JAX: the shapes are static, so the whole chain of N contractions is compiled once per (N, chi)."""
    psi = B[0][0]                                          # (2, chi): left boundary leg fixed to 0
    for j in range(1, B.shape[0]):
        psi = jnp.tensordot(psi, B[j], axes=([-1], [0]))
    return psi[..., 0]                                     # right boundary leg fixed to 0

def product_mps(spec, chi):
    """Product state |spec[0]> |spec[1]> ... as a padded MPS  (B[N,chi,2,chi], lam[N+1,chi]).
    MATH   B[j][0, s, 0] = v_j[s], all other entries zero;  every cut has the single Schmidt value 1.
    '0' = spin up (Z=+1), '1' = spin down (Z=-1), '+'/'-' = eigenstates of X."""
    N = len(spec)
    B = np.zeros((N, chi, 2, chi), dtype=complex)
    for j, c in enumerate(spec):
        v = np.array(_SPIN_MPS[c], dtype=complex)
        B[j, 0, :, 0] = v / np.linalg.norm(v)
    lam = np.zeros((N + 1, chi))
    lam[:, 0] = 1.0
    return jnp.asarray(B, dtype=CDTYPE), jnp.asarray(lam, dtype=RDTYPE)

def mps_expect_sites(B, lam, O):
    """<O_j> for ALL sites j at once.   MATH  theta_j = lam[j] B[j];  <O_j> = sum theta*[a,t,b] O[t,s] theta[a,s,b].
    COST O(N chi^2).  The letter j in the einsum is a batch index (one independent contraction per site)."""
    theta = lam[:-1, :, None, None] * B
    return jnp.real(jnp.einsum("jatb,ts,jasb->j", jnp.conj(theta), jnp.asarray(O, dtype=B.dtype), theta))

def mps_overlap(B_phi, B_psi):
    """<phi|psi> of two padded MPS (bond dimensions may differ) by a transfer-matrix sweep.
    MATH  E_0 = e_0 e_0^T (dummy left bonds);  E <- sum_s conj(Bphi^s)^T E Bpsi^s ;  result E[0,0].
    JAX   all site tensors have the same shape -> the sweep is a lax.scan over the site axis.  COST O(N chi^3)."""
    E0 = jnp.zeros((B_phi.shape[1], B_psi.shape[1]), dtype=B_psi.dtype).at[0, 0].set(1.0)

    def push(E, tensors):
        Bp, Bq = tensors
        return jnp.einsum("ac,asb,csd->bd", E, jnp.conj(Bp), Bq), None

    E, _ = lax.scan(push, E0, (B_phi, B_psi))
    return E[0, 0]

def mps_correlator(B, lam, O, i, P, j):
    """<O_i P_j> for i < j (static ints) with a transfer-matrix sweep between the two sites.  COST O((j-i) chi^3).
    O and P must be Hermitian: the product O_i P_j on two different sites is then Hermitian and its expectation value
    real, which is what is returned (the real part; a non-Hermitian pair such as sigma+ sigma- would lose its
    imaginary part).  i >= j raises ValueError (i == j is the single-site <O P>, use mps_expect_sites)."""
    i, j = int(i), int(j)
    if not 0 <= i < j < B.shape[0]:                                   # j <= i silently returned a wrong number
        raise ValueError(f"mps_correlator needs 0 <= i < j < {B.shape[0]}, got i={i}, j={j}")
    O, P = jnp.asarray(O, dtype=B.dtype), jnp.asarray(P, dtype=B.dtype)
    theta = lam[i][:, None, None] * B[i]
    E = jnp.einsum("atb,ts,asc->bc", jnp.conj(theta), O, theta)       # open legs: right bonds (bra b, ket c)
    for k in range(i + 1, j):
        E = jnp.einsum("bc,bsd,cse->de", E, jnp.conj(B[k]), B[k])     # push through site k
    return jnp.real(jnp.einsum("bc,btd,ts,csd->", E, jnp.conj(B[j]), P, B[j]))

def xxz_mpo(N, Jxx=1.0, Jyy=1.0, Jzz=1.0, hx=0.0, hz=0.0):
    """MPO of H = sum_i (Jxx X X + Jyy Y Y + Jzz Z Z)_{i,i+1} + sum_i (hx X_i + hz Z_i), open chain, Pauli convention.

    RETURNS  W of shape (N, D, D, 2, 2), D = 5:  W[j, v, w, s_out, s_in] = the operator at site j for the move v -> w.
             States of the finite-state machine: 4 = nothing placed, 1/2/3 = X/Y/Z placed on the previous site, 0 = complete.
             Site 0 keeps only row 4 and site N-1 only column 0 (all other entries zero), so all sites have the same shape.
    CONVENTION (used by every MPO routine: mpo_to_dense, mpo_expectation, boundary_environments, dmrg)
             the START state of the machine is the LAST index D-1 and the END state is index 0, for any D.
    """
    D = 5
    W = jnp.zeros((D, D, 2, 2), dtype=CDTYPE)
    W = W.at[0, 0].set(I2).at[4, 4].set(I2)                                 # stay: identities
    W = W.at[1, 0].set(X).at[2, 0].set(Y).at[3, 0].set(Z)                   # second half of a bond -> complete
    W = W.at[4, 1].set(Jxx * X).at[4, 2].set(Jyy * Y).at[4, 3].set(Jzz * Z)  # first half of a bond
    W = W.at[4, 0].set(hx * X + hz * Z)                                     # a single-site term, complete at once
    Ws = jnp.stack([W] * N)
    Ws = Ws.at[0].set(jnp.zeros_like(W).at[4].set(W[4]))                   # left boundary: only row 4
    Ws = Ws.at[N - 1].set(jnp.zeros_like(W).at[:, 0].set(W[:, 0]))         # right boundary: only column 0
    return Ws

def left_env_update(L, A, W):
    """L_{j+1}[b,w,b'] = sum L_j[a,v,a'] A[a,s,b] W[v,w,t,s] conj(A)[a',t,b']

    Letters  a v x = L_j (x = a', the bra bond);  a s b = A (ket);  v w t s = W (t = s_out, s = s_in);  x t c = conj(A) (bra, c = b').
             The BRA tensor carries the OUTPUT leg of W, because <psi|H|psi> = sum conj(A) (W A).
    """
    return jnp.einsum("avx,asb,vwts,xtc->bwc", L, A, W, jnp.conj(A))

def right_env_update(R, B, W):
    """R_j[a,v,a'] = sum B[a,s,b] W[v,w,t,s] conj(B)[a',t,b'] R_{j+1}[b,w,b']

    Letters  a s b = B (ket);  v w t s = W (t = s_out, s = s_in);  x t c = conj(B) (bra, x = a', c = b');  b w c = R_{j+1}.
    """
    return jnp.einsum("asb,vwts,xtc,bwc->avx", B, W, jnp.conj(B), R)

def mpo_expectation(B, W):
    """<psi|H|psi> of a normalised MPS (any gauge) for an MPO: contract all sites with right_env_update and read R_0[0, D-1, 0]."""
    chi, D = B.shape[1], W.shape[1]
    R = jnp.zeros((chi, D, chi), dtype=CDTYPE).at[0, 0, 0].set(1.0)
    for j in range(B.shape[0] - 1, -1, -1):
        R = right_env_update(R, B[j], W[j])
    return jnp.real(R[0, D - 1, 0])

def boundary_environments(chi, D):
    """L_0 and R_N in the padded format (chi, D, chi): L_0[0, D-1, 0] = 1 (nothing placed), R_N[0, 0, 0] = 1 (complete)."""
    L0 = jnp.zeros((chi, D, chi), dtype=CDTYPE).at[0, D - 1, 0].set(1.0)
    RN = jnp.zeros((chi, D, chi), dtype=CDTYPE).at[0, 0, 0].set(1.0)
    return L0, RN

def heff_apply(L, W1, W2, R, theta):
    """(H_eff theta)[a',s',t',b'] = sum L[a,v,a'] W1[v,w,s',s] W2[w,u,t',t] R[b,u,b'] theta[a,s,t,b]
    Letters: a v x = L (x = a'), v w y s = W1 (y = s'), w u z t = W2 (z = t'), b u q = R (q = b'), a s t b = theta.
    COST O(chi^3 D d^2) with the contraction order chosen by optimize="optimal"; the (4 chi^2)^2 matrix is never formed."""
    return jnp.einsum("avx,vwys,wuzt,buq,astb->xyzq", L, W1, W2, R, theta, optimize="optimal")

def lanczos_lowest(matvec, v0, m, mask=None, key=None):
    """Lowest eigenpair (E, x) of a Hermitian operator given only matvec, from m Lanczos steps started at v0.

    MATH    beta_j v_{j+1} = H v_j - alpha_j v_j - beta_{j-1} v_{j-1}  (Lanczos recurrence), full reorthogonalisation (twice);
            Ritz vector x = sum_j s_j v_j of the lowest eigenvector s of the tridiagonal T.
    BREAKDOWN  beta_j <= 1e-12 ||H||, with ||H|| estimated by the largest ||H v_j|| seen (a RELATIVE test: the rounding
            residual of an exhausted Krylov space grows with ||H||).  The Krylov space K is then invariant, but it need
            not contain the lowest eigenvector (v0 orthogonal to it, e.g. v0 an eigenvector or in another symmetry
            sector).  Lanczos therefore RESTARTS from a random vector of the allowed space (`mask`), orthogonalised
            against K, and writes beta_j = 0 into T: T becomes block diagonal and every Ritz value belongs to H.  Only if
            nothing is left (the whole allowed space is exhausted) do the remaining rows of T get a diagonal far above
            the spectrum, so that their spurious eigenvalues cannot be the lowest.
    ARGUMENTS  mask (shape of v0, 0/1, optional): the subspace H acts on; the restart vectors are drawn inside it.
            key: PRNG key of the restart vectors (default PRNGKey(0): deterministic).
    """
    shape = v0.shape
    v0 = (v0 / jnp.linalg.norm(v0)).reshape(-1)
    mask = jnp.ones(v0.size, dtype=RDTYPE) if mask is None else jnp.asarray(mask, dtype=RDTYPE).reshape(-1)
    key = jax.random.PRNGKey(0) if key is None else key
    V0 = jnp.zeros((m, v0.size), dtype=v0.dtype).at[0].set(v0)

    def orthogonalise(w, V):                                     # w <- w - sum_i v_i <v_i|w>, twice (rounding); V holds the v_i as ROWS
        w = w - jnp.conj(V @ jnp.conj(w)) @ V
        return w - jnp.conj(V @ jnp.conj(w)) @ V

    def body(carry, j):
        V, v, v_prev, beta_prev, alive, hnorm = carry
        Hv = matvec(v.reshape(shape)).reshape(-1)
        hnorm = jnp.maximum(hnorm, jnp.linalg.norm(Hv))          # lower bound of ||H||: the scale of the breakdown test
        alpha = jnp.real(jnp.vdot(v, Hv))
        w = orthogonalise(Hv - alpha * v - beta_prev * v_prev, V)
        beta = jnp.linalg.norm(w)
        cont = beta > 1e-12 * hnorm                              # False: breakdown, K is an invariant subspace

        def restart(_):                                          # random vector of the allowed space, orthogonal to K
            r = mask * jax.random.normal(jax.random.fold_in(key, j), (v.size,), dtype=RDTYPE)
            r = orthogonalise(r.astype(V.dtype), V)
            rn = jnp.linalg.norm(r)
            fresh = rn > 1e-8 * jnp.sqrt(jnp.sum(mask))          # False: the allowed space is exhausted
            return r / jnp.where(fresh, rn, 1.0), fresh

        v_next, fresh = lax.cond(cont, lambda _: (w / jnp.where(cont, beta, 1.0), jnp.array(True)), restart, None)
        v_next = jnp.where(alive & (cont | fresh), v_next, 0.0)
        b_out = jnp.where(alive & cont, beta, 0.0)               # a restart decouples the new block: T_{j,j+1} = 0
        V = V.at[j + 1].set(v_next, mode="drop")                 # at j = m-1 the index j+1 is out of range; "drop" discards that write
        return (V, v_next, v, b_out, alive & (cont | fresh), hnorm), (alpha, b_out, alive)

    init = (V0, v0, jnp.zeros_like(v0), jnp.zeros((), dtype=RDTYPE), jnp.array(True), jnp.zeros((), dtype=RDTYPE))
    (V, _, _, _, _, hnorm), (alphas, betas, used) = lax.scan(body, init, jnp.arange(m))
    alphas = jnp.where(used, alphas, 1e3 * (1.0 + hnorm))      # unused rows of T: far above every Ritz value
    evals_T, evecs_T = jnp.linalg.eigh(jnp.diag(alphas) + jnp.diag(betas[:-1], 1) + jnp.diag(betas[:-1], -1))   # Ritz values and vectors of T
    x = evecs_T[:, 0].astype(V.dtype) @ V                    # Ritz vector of the lowest Ritz value, in the big space
    return evals_T[0], (x / jnp.linalg.norm(x)).reshape(shape)

def split_two_site(theta, chi, move_right, support=None):
    """theta[a,s,t,b] (padded, shape (chi,2,2,chi)) -> two tensors (chi,2,chi), Schmidt values, discarded weight.

    MATH   theta_{(as),(tb)} = U S V^dag, keep chi values:  S <- S[:chi]/||S[:chi]||,  eps = sum_{k>=chi} S_k^2 / sum_k S_k^2
           move_right:  left = U (left-canonical),     right = S V^dag (carries the centre)
           move_left :  left = U S (carries centre),   right = V^dag (right-canonical)
    COMPLETION  The columns of U (move_right) or rows of V^dag (move_left) whose Schmidt value vanishes (< LAM_CUT) are
           replaced by an orthonormal completion INSIDE the space of genuine block states, P = {(a,s): support[a]}
           (move_right) or {(t,b): support[b]} (move_left): the orthonormal basis of (1 - U_k U_k^dag) P.  These
           directions carry weight zero now; a later step can give them weight, which is how the bond dimension grows
           faster than the factor 2 per half sweep allowed by the Schmidt rank alone.  Columns beyond dim P are EXACT
           zeros.  The completion that LAPACK returns would mix in padded (zero-norm) block "states"; H_eff, which
           assumes orthonormal block bases, then has a spurious eigenvalue 0 on them (see dmrg).
    ARGUMENTS  chi must equal theta.shape[0] (the storage size): the routine always keeps exactly chi of the 2*chi values.
           support (chi,) bool: the bond indices of the outer bond (a for move_right, b for move_left) that are block
           states (the others are padding); default: all.
    """
    U, S, Vh = jnp.linalg.svd(theta.reshape(2 * chi, 2 * chi), full_matrices=False)
    weight = S ** 2
    eps = jnp.sum(weight[chi:]) / jnp.sum(weight)
    S = S[:chi] / jnp.sqrt(jnp.sum(weight[:chi]))
    keep = S > LAM_CUT                                       # a prefix: S is sorted
    S = jnp.where(keep, S, 0.0)
    support = jnp.ones(chi, dtype=bool) if support is None else support
    if move_right:
        Q, p = U[:, :chi], jnp.repeat(support, 2)            # isometry U: rows (a, s), allowed rows support[a]
    else:
        Q, p = jnp.conj(Vh[:chi]).T, jnp.tile(support, 2)    # isometry V: rows (t, b), allowed rows support[b]
    p = p.astype(Q.dtype)
    Qk = Q * keep[None, :] * p[:, None]                      # kept vectors (exactly zero outside P)
    C = jnp.diag(p) - Qk @ (jnp.conj(Qk).T * p[None, :])     # (1 - Qk Qk^dag) P: its range is the completion space
    Uc, sc, _ = jnp.linalg.svd(C)
    Uc = Uc * (sc > 0.5)[None, :]                            # singular values are 1 (completion) or 0 (nothing): drop the 0s
    r = jnp.sum(keep)
    fill = jnp.take(Uc, jnp.clip(jnp.arange(chi) - r, 0, 2 * chi - 1), axis=1)   # completion vector k - r into column k >= r
    Q = jnp.where(keep[None, :], Qk, fill)
    if move_right:
        return Q.reshape(chi, 2, chi), (S[:, None] * Vh[:chi] * keep[:, None]).reshape(chi, 2, chi), S, eps
    return (U[:, :chi] * S[None, :]).reshape(chi, 2, chi), jnp.conj(Q).T.reshape(chi, 2, chi), S, eps

_LOCAL_STEP_CACHE = {}                                             # one XLA compilation per (chi, m, direction)
def make_local_step(chi, m, move_right):
    """Compiled DMRG step on the bond (j, j+1): contract, solve H_eff theta = E theta (Lanczos, m steps), split.

    The compiled step depends only on (chi, m, direction) -- never on the site, the Hamiltonian or the field -- so
    it is built once and cached.  Without the cache every call of `dmrg`, hence every point of a parameter scan,
    pays a fresh XLA compilation, which dominates the run time although the arithmetic is identical."""
    key = (chi, m, move_right)
    if key in _LOCAL_STEP_CACHE:
        return _LOCAL_STEP_CACHE[key]

    @jax.jit
    def step(L, W1, W2, R, M1, M2):
        theta = jnp.einsum("asm,mtb->astb", M1, M2)                                   # 1. two-site tensor
        used_L, used_R = jnp.any(L != 0, axis=(0, 1)), jnp.any(R != 0, axis=(0, 1))   #    bond indices that are block states
        mask = used_L[:, None, None, None] & used_R[None, None, None, :] & jnp.ones(theta.shape, dtype=bool)
        E, theta = lanczos_lowest(lambda t: heff_apply(L, W1, W2, R, t), theta, m, mask)   # 2. local ground state
        left, right, S, eps = split_two_site(theta, chi, move_right, used_L if move_right else used_R)   # 3. split, move the centre
        return left, right, S, eps, E

    _LOCAL_STEP_CACHE[key] = step
    return step

def mps_recanonicalise(B):
    """Exact right-canonical form and the TRUE Schmidt values of every cut, for a padded MPS whose site 0 carries the norm.

    MATH
        Left sweep  j = 0..N-2:   A[j]_{(a s), b} = Q R   (QR);   A[j] <- Q (left-canonical);   A[j+1] <- R A[j+1].
        After it, sites 0..N-2 are left-canonical and the whole norm sits on site N-1.
        Right sweep j = N-1..1:   A[j]_{a, (s b)} = U S V^dag   (SVD);   B[j] <- V^dag;   lam[j] <- S;   A[j-1] <- A[j-1] U S.
        At step j everything left of the cut is left-canonical and everything right of it right-canonical, so
        S are exactly the Schmidt values of the cut left of site j (no stored value from an earlier, different state).
        No truncation can occur: every matrix has rank <= chi, and all chi values are kept.  lam[0] = lam[N] = (1,0,..).
        Singular values below LAM_CUT * S_max (rounding zeros) are set to exact zero with their rows of V^dag, so that the
        padding of the returned tensors is exactly zero (the completion LAPACK returns there is arbitrary).
    WHY    `dmrg` records lam[j] at the split of bond j during the right-to-left half sweep; the later steps at bonds
           j-1, ..., 1 change the left block of that cut, so the recorded values describe an intermediate state.
           Likewise `state_to_mps` with truncation stores lam[j] before cut j-1 is truncated.  This pass makes
           (B, lam) consistent, so that the lam-based observables (mps_expect_sites, mps_expect_bonds, mps_entropies,
           mps_correlator) are those of the state that B encodes.  The state itself is unchanged (to rounding).
    ARGUMENTS  B (N, chi, 2, chi) any padded MPS whose left boundary leg is index 0 and right boundary leg index 0.
    RETURNS    B (N, chi, 2, chi) right-canonical on sites 1..N-1 (site 0 = centre, holds the norm), lam (N+1, chi).
    COST       N QR and N SVD of (2chi x chi) matrices: O(N chi^3).
    """
    N, chi = B.shape[0], B.shape[1]
    A = list(B)
    for j in range(N - 1):                                             # left sweep: QR, push R to the right
        Q, R = jnp.linalg.qr(A[j].reshape(chi * 2, chi))
        A[j] = Q.reshape(chi, 2, chi)
        A[j + 1] = jnp.einsum("ab,bsc->asc", R, A[j + 1])
    lam = jnp.zeros((N + 1, chi), dtype=RDTYPE).at[0, 0].set(1.0).at[N, 0].set(1.0)
    for j in range(N - 1, 0, -1):                                      # right sweep: SVD, push U S to the left
        U, S, Vh = jnp.linalg.svd(A[j].reshape(chi, 2 * chi), full_matrices=False)
        keep = S > LAM_CUT * S[0]                                      # beyond the rank: exact zeros (see split_two_site)
        S, Vh = jnp.where(keep, S, 0.0), Vh * keep[:, None]
        A[j] = Vh.reshape(chi, 2, chi)
        lam = lam.at[j].set(S.astype(RDTYPE))
        A[j - 1] = jnp.einsum("asb,bc->asc", A[j - 1], U * S[None, :].astype(U.dtype))
    A[0] = A[0].at[1:].set(0.0)                                        # unused rows of the dummy left bond: exactly zero
    return jnp.stack(A), lam

def dmrg(W, M, chi, n_sweeps, m=20, trace=False, check_convergence=True):
    """Two-site DMRG for the MPO W (N, D, D, 2, 2), starting from the padded MPS M (N, chi, 2, chi).

    RETURNS  B (N, chi, 2, chi) right-canonical, lam (N+1, chi) Schmidt values of all bonds, history list of
             (sweep, direction, bond j, energy, discarded weight, number of non-zero Schmidt values) for every local step.
    FINAL PASS  The S of the split at bond j+1 is recorded during the right-to-left half sweep, but the later steps
             at bonds j, ..., 0 change the left block of that cut, so the recorded values belong to an intermediate
             state; they are exact only after convergence at a chi where nothing is truncated.  `dmrg` therefore ends
             with `mps_recanonicalise` (exact, O(N chi^3), state unchanged), which recomputes lam from the returned B.
    START    M is first brought to right-canonical form by `mps_recanonicalise` and normalised (any gauge and norm are
             accepted; the padding becomes exact zeros).  A zero or non-finite start raises ValueError.
    SAFETY   The local problem H_eff theta = E theta is the projected one only if every bond index is an orthonormal
             block state or exactly zero (`split_two_site`); otherwise zero-norm directions add a spurious eigenvalue 0,
             which is the lowest one whenever the physical local energies are positive (e.g. a Neel start of the
             ferromagnetic TFIM), and the state collapses to the zero vector.  A local step whose Schmidt values are not
             normalised or whose energy is not finite, and a final state whose norm is not 1, raise RuntimeError.
    SYMMETRY Lanczos keeps the symmetry sector of its start vector (e.g. S^z_tot for an XXZ chain).  On a breakdown it
             restarts from a random vector (`lanczos_lowest`), which happens on the short bonds near the chain ends and
             for an eigenstate start; this lets the state leave the sector of the start, but nothing guarantees it.
    CONVERGENCE  The sweeps stop after n_sweeps.  With check_convergence, the energy change dE of the last sweep is
             compared with the truncation error of the energy, which is about eps_max |E| (eps_max = largest discarded
             weight of the last sweep): the run counts as converged if |dE| <= max(10 eps_max |E|, 100 eps_mach |E|),
             the second term being the rounding of E.  Otherwise one line is printed (not raised).  A single sweep
             has no energy change to compare and is reported as not checked.
    """
    N, D = W.shape[0], W.shape[1]
    M, _ = mps_recanonicalise(jnp.asarray(M, dtype=CDTYPE))        # right-canonical, padding exactly zero
    nrm = float(jnp.linalg.norm(M[0]))                             # site 0 carries the norm
    if not np.isfinite(nrm) or nrm < 1e-12:
        raise ValueError(f"dmrg: the start MPS has norm {nrm:.3e} (zero or not finite)")
    M = M.at[0].set(M[0] / nrm)
    tol = float(jnp.sqrt(jnp.finfo(RDTYPE).eps))                   # 1.5e-8 (double), 3.5e-4 (single)

    def check(S):                                                  # raise instead of continuing with a broken step
        h, norm2 = history[-1], float(jnp.sum(S ** 2))
        if not (np.isfinite(h[3]) and abs(norm2 - 1.0) < tol):
            raise RuntimeError(f"dmrg: local step (sweep {h[0]}, {h[1]}, bond {h[2]}) gave E = {h[3]}, sum S^2 = {norm2}")

    step_right, step_left = make_local_step(chi, m, True), make_local_step(chi, m, False)
    L, R = [None] * (N + 1), [None] * (N + 1)
    L[0], R[N] = boundary_environments(chi, D)
    for j in range(N - 1, 1, -1):                                  # right environments of the initial state
        R[j] = right_env_update(R[j + 1], M[j], W[j])
    M = list(M)
    lam = jnp.zeros((N + 1, chi), dtype=RDTYPE).at[0, 0].set(1.0).at[N, 0].set(1.0)
    history = []
    for sweep in range(n_sweeps):
        for j in range(0, N - 1):                                  # ---- left -> right
            M[j], M[j + 1], S, eps, E = step_right(L[j], W[j], W[j + 1], R[j + 2], M[j], M[j + 1])
            L[j + 1] = left_env_update(L[j], M[j], W[j])           # the only environment that changes
            history.append((sweep, "->", j, float(E), float(eps), int(jnp.sum(S > 1e-12))))
            check(S)
        for j in range(N - 2, -1, -1):                             # ---- right -> left
            M[j], M[j + 1], S, eps, E = step_left(L[j], W[j], W[j + 1], R[j + 2], M[j], M[j + 1])
            R[j + 1] = right_env_update(R[j + 2], M[j + 1], W[j + 1])
            lam = lam.at[j + 1].set(S)                             # Schmidt values of the bond left of site j+1
            history.append((sweep, "<-", j, float(E), float(eps), int(jnp.sum(S > 1e-12))))
            check(S)
        if trace:
            last = [h for h in history if h[0] == sweep]
            print(f"   sweep {sweep}:  E = {last[-1][3]:.12f}   largest discarded weight {max(h[4] for h in last):.1e}   "
                  f"largest bond dimension {max(h[5] for h in last)}")
    B, lam = mps_recanonicalise(jnp.stack(M))                     # the lam recorded above is stale: recompute it from B
    nrm = float(jnp.linalg.norm(B[0]))
    if not abs(nrm - 1.0) < tol:
        raise RuntimeError(f"dmrg: the final MPS has norm {nrm:.3e} instead of 1")
    if check_convergence:                                          # energy change of the last sweep vs its truncation
        if n_sweeps < 2:
            print(f"dmrg: convergence not checked after {n_sweeps} sweep(s) (it needs the energy change between two); "
                  "run more sweeps")
        else:
            E, dE = history[-1][3], history[-1][3] - [h[3] for h in history if h[0] == n_sweeps - 2][-1]
            eps = max(h[4] for h in history if h[0] == n_sweeps - 1)
            if abs(dE) > max(10 * eps * abs(E), 100 * float(jnp.finfo(RDTYPE).eps) * abs(E)):
                print(f"dmrg: not converged after {n_sweeps} sweeps (last-sweep energy change {dE:.1e} vs largest "
                      f"discarded weight {eps:.1e}); run more sweeps")
    return B, lam, history

# Born machine (derived with the MPS Born machine): SVD split, normalisation Z by transfer matrices, explicit state (validation),
# per-snapshot environments, bond amplitudes, NLL gradient, great-circle line search, bond update and training loop,
# log-amplitudes and NLL of a set of snapshots, total variation distance.
@partial(jax.jit, static_argnames=("chi_out", "move_right"))
def svd_split(B, chi_out, cutoff, move_right, chi_cap=None):
    """Split a two-site tensor B[a, s, t, b] of the Born machine by an SVD of the matrix B[(a s), (t b)].

    MATH            B = U diag(lambda) V^T;  keep k with lambda_k > cutoff * lambda_0 and k < chi_cap (zero the rest)
                    move_right: A_j = U (left-canonical),  A_{j+1} = diag(lambda) V^T (new centre)
                    move left:  A_j = U diag(lambda) (new centre),  A_{j+1} = V^T (right-canonical)
                    the centre is divided by its norm, so the normalisation Z = sum_s phi(s)^2 is 1 afterwards
    ARGUMENTS       chi_out: array size of the outputs (static, the padding width); chi_cap: maximal bond dimension
                    chi_max (traced, so one compilation serves every cap), default chi_out
    RETURNS         A_j (chi_out, 2, chi_out), A_{j+1}, number of kept values, discarded weight
                    eps = sum_{dropped} lambda_k^2 / sum_k lambda_k^2
    """
    chi_cap = chi_out if chi_cap is None else chi_cap
    a, s, t, b = B.shape
    U, lam, Vt = jnp.linalg.svd(B.reshape(a * s, t * b), full_matrices=False)
    keep = (lam > cutoff * lam[0]) & (jnp.arange(lam.shape[0]) < chi_cap)
    lam_k = jnp.where(keep, lam, 0.0)
    eps = jnp.sum(jnp.where(keep, 0.0, lam ** 2)) / jnp.sum(lam ** 2)
    if move_right:
        A1 = (U[:, :chi_out] * keep[:chi_out]).reshape(a, s, chi_out)
        A2 = (lam_k[:chi_out, None] * Vt[:chi_out]).reshape(chi_out, t, b)
        A2 = A2 / jnp.linalg.norm(A2)
    else:
        A1 = (U[:, :chi_out] * lam_k[None, :chi_out]).reshape(a, s, chi_out)
        A1 = A1 / jnp.linalg.norm(A1)
        A2 = (Vt[:chi_out] * keep[:chi_out, None]).reshape(chi_out, t, b)
    return A1, A2, jnp.sum(keep), eps


@jax.jit
def log_Z_transfer(As):
    """ln Z, Z = sum_s phi(s)^2, by transfer matrices, rescaled at every site (the normalisation of P = phi^2 / Z).

    MATH            E <- sum_s A^{s T} E A^s  (einsum "ab,asc,bsd->cd"),  E / |E|,  lnZ += ln |E|;   Z = E[0, 0] at the end
    COST            O(N chi^3), valid for any MPS (canonical or not)
    """
    E = jnp.zeros((As.shape[1], As.shape[1]), RDTYPE).at[0, 0].set(1.0)

    def body(carry, A):
        E, lz = carry
        E = jnp.einsum("ab,asc,bsd->cd", E, A, A)
        nE = jnp.linalg.norm(E)
        return (E / nE, lz + jnp.log(nE)), None
    (E, lz), _ = lax.scan(body, (E, jnp.zeros((), RDTYPE)), As)
    return lz + jnp.log(E[0, 0])


def dense_state(As):
    """Contract the padded MPS into the explicit vector phi of length 2^N (NumPy; validation only, small N)."""
    As = np.asarray(As)
    T = As[0][0]                                                   # (2, chi): left boundary row 0
    for A in As[1:]:
        T = np.tensordot(T, A, axes=([-1], [0]))
    return T[..., 0].reshape(-1)                                   # right boundary column 0; bit 0 most significant


def onehot_sites(v):
    """snapshots v (M, N) -> one-hot (N, M, 2), site-major: onehot[j, m] = (1 - v_mj, v_mj)."""
    return jnp.swapaxes(jax.nn.one_hot(v, 2, dtype=RDTYPE), 0, 1)


@jax.jit
def env_step_left(L, A, oh):
    """l'[n, b] = sum_{a,s} l[n, a] oh[n, s] A[a, s, b] for every snapshot n, each row then divided by its norm.

    RETURNS  normalised l', ln |l'| per snapshot (-inf where the amplitude is exactly zero)
    """
    L = jnp.einsum("na,ns,asb->nb", L, oh, A)
    nrm = jnp.linalg.norm(L, axis=1)
    return L / jnp.where(nrm > 0, nrm, 1.0)[:, None], jnp.log(nrm)


@jax.jit
def env_step_right(R, A, oh):
    """r'[n, a] = sum_{s,b} A[a, s, b] oh[n, s] r[n, b] for every snapshot n, each row then divided by its norm."""
    R = jnp.einsum("asb,ns,nb->na", A, oh, R)
    nrm = jnp.linalg.norm(R, axis=1)
    return R / jnp.where(nrm > 0, nrm, 1.0)[:, None], jnp.log(nrm)


def pair_onehot(oh_j, oh_k):
    """One-hot vector e_v of the bit pair (v_j, v_{j+1}), index p = 2 s + t:  (M, 4)."""
    return (oh_j[:, :, None] * oh_k[:, None, :]).reshape(-1, 4)


def bond_amplitudes(B, L, oh_j, oh_k, R):
    """phi[n] = sum_{a,b} l[n, a] B[a, v_j, v_{j+1}, b] r[n, b] for every snapshot n (up to the stored log-norms)."""
    a, _, _, b = B.shape
    Bp = jnp.transpose(B.reshape(a, 4, b), (1, 0, 2))                  # (4, a, b): block p = (s, t)
    LB = jnp.einsum("na,pab->npb", L, Bp)
    return jnp.einsum("npb,np,nb->n", LB, pair_onehot(oh_j, oh_k), R)


def nll_gradient(B, L, oh_j, oh_k, R):
    """Gradient of the NLL with respect to the bond tensor (derived with the MPS Born machine):
    Gamma = 2 B / Z - (2 / N_T) sum_n l_n (x) e_{v_j} (x) e_{v_j+1} (x) r_n / phi_n,  Z = |B|^2,  N_T training snapshots.

    IMPLEMENTATION  the data term is one matrix product: sum_n (e_n (x) l_n / phi_n) r_n^T, einsum "npa,nb->pab"
    """
    a, s, t, b = B.shape
    psi = bond_amplitudes(B, L, oh_j, oh_k, R)
    W = pair_onehot(oh_j, oh_k)[:, :, None] * (L / psi[:, None])[:, None, :]        # (N_T, 4, a)
    data = jnp.transpose(jnp.einsum("npa,nb->pab", W, R), (1, 0, 2)).reshape(a, s, t, b)
    return 2 * B / jnp.sum(B ** 2) - 2 * data / L.shape[0]


THETAS = jnp.concatenate([jnp.zeros(1, RDTYPE), (jnp.pi / 4) * 2.0 ** -jnp.arange(26.0, dtype=RDTYPE)])


def great_circle_step(B, L, oh_j, oh_k, R):
    """One step on the great circle B(theta) = cos(theta) B - sin(theta) Gamma_hat, theta from the grid THETAS.

    MATH            B <- B/|B|;  Gamma_hat = Gamma/|Gamma|;  phi_theta = cos(theta) phi - sin(theta) gamma;
                    cost(theta) = -mean_n ln phi_theta[n]^2   (Z(theta) = 1);   theta* = argmin over the grid (0 included)
    """
    B = B / jnp.linalg.norm(B)
    psi = bond_amplitudes(B, L, oh_j, oh_k, R)
    Gam = nll_gradient(B, L, oh_j, oh_k, R)
    nG = jnp.linalg.norm(Gam)
    Gam_hat = Gam / jnp.where(nG > 0, nG, 1.0)          # Gamma = 0 exactly (bits that are 0 in every example): no step
    gam = bond_amplitudes(Gam_hat, L, oh_j, oh_k, R)
    cos_t, sin_t = jnp.cos(THETAS), jnp.sin(THETAS)
    cost = -jnp.mean(jnp.log((cos_t[:, None] * psi[None] - sin_t[:, None] * gam[None]) ** 2), axis=1)
    k = jnp.argmin(cost)
    return cos_t[k] * B - sin_t[k] * Gam_hat


def bond_update(A_j, A_k, L, lnL, R, lnR, oh_j, oh_k, chi_cap, cutoff, n_steps, move_right):
    """One bond of a sweep: merge (B = A_j A_{j+1}), n_steps line-search steps, SVD split, new environment.

    RETURNS  A_j, A_{j+1}, new environment (l of site j+1 moving right, r of site j+1 moving left) and its log-norm,
             NLL of the training snapshots after the split (Z = 1), kept bond dimension, discarded weight
    """
    B = jnp.einsum("asm,mtb->astb", A_j, A_k)
    B, _ = lax.scan(lambda B, _: (great_circle_step(B, L, oh_j, oh_k, R), None), B, None, length=n_steps)
    A1, A2, kept, eps = svd_split(B, A_j.shape[0], cutoff, move_right, chi_cap)
    psi = bond_amplitudes(jnp.einsum("asm,mtb->astb", A1, A2), L, oh_j, oh_k, R)
    nll = -jnp.mean(2 * (jnp.log(jnp.abs(psi)) + lnL + lnR))
    if move_right:
        env, ln_env = env_step_left(L, A1, oh_j)
        ln_env = ln_env + lnL
    else:
        env, ln_env = env_step_right(R, A2, oh_k)
        ln_env = ln_env + lnR
    return A1, A2, env, ln_env, nll, kept, eps


@jax.jit
def log_psi(As, v):
    """ln |phi(v)| for snapshots v (M, N) of a real MPS, contracted from the left with per-snapshot rescaling (O(N chi^2) each)."""
    oh = onehot_sites(v)
    L0 = jnp.zeros((v.shape[0], As.shape[1]), RDTYPE).at[:, 0].set(1.0)

    def body(carry, inp):
        L, lz = carry
        L, ln = env_step_left(L, *inp)
        return (L, lz + ln), None
    (L, lz), _ = lax.scan(body, (L0, jnp.zeros(v.shape[0], RDTYPE)), (As, oh))
    return lz + jnp.log(jnp.abs(L[:, 0]))


def nll_of(As, v):
    """NLL = -(1/M) sum_m ln P(v_m), P = phi^2 / Z, for the snapshots v, with Z from log_Z_transfer.

    RETURNS  mean NLL over the snapshots with P > 0, number of snapshots with P = 0, array of -ln P (finite entries)
    """
    lp = 2 * np.asarray(log_psi(As, v)) - float(log_Z_transfer(As))
    ok = np.isfinite(lp)
    return float(-lp[ok].mean()), int((~ok).sum()), -lp[ok]


@jax.jit
def initial_right_envs(As, oh):
    """r_j (environment of the sites >= j) and its log-norm for j = 1 .. N-1, by a reverse lax.scan."""
    e0 = jnp.zeros((oh.shape[1], As.shape[1]), RDTYPE).at[:, 0].set(1.0)

    def body(carry, inp):
        R, lnR = carry
        R, ln = env_step_right(R, *inp)
        return (R, lnR + ln), (R, lnR + ln)
    _, (Rs, lnRs) = lax.scan(body, (e0, jnp.zeros(oh.shape[1], RDTYPE)), (As[1:], oh[1:]), reverse=True)
    return Rs, lnRs


@partial(jax.jit, static_argnames=("n_steps",))
def training_loop(As, Lst, lnL, Rst, lnR, oh, chi_cap, cutoff, n_steps):
    """One loop of the Born machine: right sweep over bonds 1..N-1, then left sweep back.

    STORAGE         As (N, chi_s, 2, chi_s);  Lst[j], lnL[j]: environment of the sites < j, (N, N_T, chi_s), (N, N_T);
                    Rst[j], lnR[j]: environment of the sites >= j, (N+1, N_T, chi_s), (N+1, N_T)
    JAX             each sweep is a lax.scan over the bond index; the stacks are carried and updated with .at[].set,
                    which XLA performs in place inside the compiled loop; chi_cap is traced
    RETURNS         updated arrays and, per bond of the left sweep: NLL after the update, kept dimension, discarded weight
    """
    N = As.shape[0]

    def right(carry, j):
        As, Lst, lnL = carry
        A1, A2, env, ln_env, *_ = bond_update(As[j], As[j + 1], Lst[j], lnL[j], Rst[j + 2], lnR[j + 2], oh[j],
                                              oh[j + 1], chi_cap, cutoff, n_steps, True)
        return (As.at[j].set(A1).at[j + 1].set(A2), Lst.at[j + 1].set(env), lnL.at[j + 1].set(ln_env)), None
    (As, Lst, lnL), _ = lax.scan(right, (As, Lst, lnL), jnp.arange(N - 1))

    def left(carry, j):
        As, Rst, lnR = carry
        A1, A2, env, ln_env, nll, kept, eps = bond_update(As[j], As[j + 1], Lst[j], lnL[j], Rst[j + 2], lnR[j + 2],
                                                          oh[j], oh[j + 1], chi_cap, cutoff, n_steps, False)
        return (As.at[j].set(A1).at[j + 1].set(A2), Rst.at[j + 1].set(env), lnR.at[j + 1].set(ln_env)), (nll, kept, eps)
    (As, Rst, lnR), out = lax.scan(left, (As, Rst, lnR), jnp.arange(N - 2, -1, -1))
    return As, Lst, lnL, Rst, lnR, out


def tv_distance(counts, p):
    """Total variation distance TV = (1/2) sum_s |counts(s) / M - p(s)| between a histogram of M draws and p."""
    return 0.5 * np.sum(np.abs(counts / counts.sum() - p))


# Classifier (derived with the MPS classifier): environments, projected inputs (the scores are linear in the bond tensor),
# cross-entropy and its gradient, gradient steps with the step size from the curvature bound, SVD splits that move
# the label with the centre, half sweeps, scores.  Here the label takes N_LABELS = 2 values (0 = ordered, 1 = disordered).
N_LABELS = 2


def left_env_step(L, A, phi_j):
    """L'[n, b] = sum_{a,s} L[n, a] phi_j[n, s] A[a, s, b]   (phi_j[n] = one-hot vector of bit j of snapshot n)."""
    return jnp.einsum("na,ns,asb->nb", L, phi_j, A)


def right_env_step(R, A, phi_j):
    """R'[n, a] = sum_{s,b} A[a, s, b] phi_j[n, s] R[n, b]."""
    return jnp.einsum("asb,ns,nb->na", A, phi_j, R)


def projected_inputs(L, phi_j, phi_k, R):
    """Phit[n, (a s t b)] = L[n, a] phi_j[n, s] phi_k[n, t] R[n, b]: the scores are linear in the bond tensor,
    F = Phit Bm;  shape (N_T, 4 chi^2)."""
    return jnp.einsum("na,ns,nt,nb->nastb", L, phi_j, phi_k, R).reshape(L.shape[0], -1)


def bond_to_matrix(Bt):
    """Bond tensor B[a, s, t, l, b] -> its matrix form Bm[(a s t b), l], one column per label."""
    return jnp.transpose(Bt, (0, 1, 2, 4, 3)).reshape(-1, Bt.shape[3])


def matrix_to_bond(Bm, shape):
    """Inverse of bond_to_matrix: Bm[(a s t b), l] -> bond tensor B[a, s, t, l, b] of the given shape."""
    a, s, t, l, b = shape
    return jnp.transpose(Bm.reshape(a, s, t, b, l), (0, 1, 2, 4, 3))


def cross_entropy(F, Y):
    """C = -(1/N_T) sum_n log softmax(F_n)[y_n], F scores (N_T, N_LABELS), Y one-hot (N_T, N_LABELS)."""
    return -jnp.mean(jnp.sum(Y * jax.nn.log_softmax(F), axis=1))


def local_gradient(Bm, Phit, Y):
    """dC/dBm = Phit^T (softmax(Phit Bm) - Y) / N_T."""
    return Phit.T @ (jax.nn.softmax(Phit @ Bm) - Y) / Phit.shape[0]


def local_update(Bt, Phit, Y, n_steps, n_power=20):
    """A few gradient steps on the bond tensor with step 1/Lambda, Lambda = lambda_max(Phit^T Phit) / (2 N_T).

    MATH            Bm <- Bm - (1/Lambda) Phit^T (softmax(Phit Bm) - Y) / N_T,   repeated n_steps times;
                    Lambda bounds the curvature of C, so a step 1/Lambda never increases C (derived with the MPS classifier)
    IMPLEMENTATION  lambda_max by power iteration v <- Phit^T Phit v / |Phit^T Phit v| (n_power steps, starting from
                    the column sums of Phit); both loops are lax.scans
    RETURNS         updated bond tensor, cost after the steps
    """
    Bm = bond_to_matrix(Bt)
    v0 = jnp.sum(Phit, axis=0)

    def power(v, _):
        w = Phit.T @ (Phit @ v)
        return w / jnp.linalg.norm(w), None
    v, _ = lax.scan(power, v0 / jnp.linalg.norm(v0), None, length=n_power)
    Lam = 0.5 * (v @ (Phit.T @ (Phit @ v))) / Phit.shape[0]

    def step(Bm, _):
        return Bm - local_gradient(Bm, Phit, Y) / Lam, None
    Bm, _ = lax.scan(step, Bm, None, length=n_steps)
    return matrix_to_bond(Bm, Bt.shape), cross_entropy(Phit @ Bm, Y)


def split_moving_right(Bt, chi):
    """B[(a s), (t l b)] = U S V^dag;  A_j = U[:, :chi] (left-canonical), centre (S V^dag)[:chi] -> site j+1.

    RETURNS  A_j (chi, 2, chi), new centre (chi, 2, N_LABELS, chi), discarded weight eps = sum_{k>=chi} S_k^2 / sum_k S_k^2
    """
    a, s, t, l, b = Bt.shape
    U, S, Vh = jnp.linalg.svd(Bt.reshape(a * s, t * l * b), full_matrices=False)
    eps = jnp.sum(S[chi:] ** 2) / jnp.sum(S ** 2)
    return U[:, :chi].reshape(a, s, chi), (S[:chi, None] * Vh[:chi]).reshape(chi, t, l, b), eps


def split_moving_left(Bt, chi):
    """B[(a s l), (t b)] = U S V^dag;  centre (U S)[:, :chi] stays on site j,  A_{j+1} = V^dag[:chi] (right-canonical)."""
    a, s, t, l, b = Bt.shape
    U, S, Vh = jnp.linalg.svd(jnp.transpose(Bt, (0, 1, 3, 2, 4)).reshape(a * s * l, t * b), full_matrices=False)
    eps = jnp.sum(S[chi:] ** 2) / jnp.sum(S ** 2)
    return (U[:, :chi] * S[None, :chi]).reshape(a, s, l, chi), Vh[:chi].reshape(chi, t, b), eps


def init_sweep_mps(key, N, chi, phiT, sigma=1e-2):
    """Right-canonical MPS near A^0 = A^1 = 1/sqrt(2), centre (label) tensor on site 0, environments of the snapshots.

    BOUNDARY  rows a != 0 of the site-0 (centre) tensor and columns b != 0 of the site-(N-1) tensor are never reached
              by a snapshot (boundary index fixed to 0); they are kept as initialised
    RETURNS   As (N, chi, 2, chi) (entry 0 unused while the centre is on site 0), centre C (chi, 2, N_LABELS, chi),
              Lst (N, N_T, chi), Rst (N+1, N_T, chi)
    """
    eye2 = jnp.stack([jnp.eye(chi, dtype=RDTYPE)] * 2, axis=1) / np.sqrt(2)
    G = eye2[None] + sigma * jax.random.normal(key, (N, chi, 2, chi), RDTYPE)
    Q, _ = jnp.linalg.qr(jnp.swapaxes(G.reshape(N, chi, 2 * chi), 1, 2))       # orthonormal columns
    As = jnp.swapaxes(Q, 1, 2).reshape(N, chi, 2, chi)                          # rows orthonormal: right-canonical
    C = jnp.einsum("asb,l->aslb", As[0], jnp.ones(N_LABELS, RDTYPE))
    n_img = phiT.shape[1]
    e0 = jnp.zeros((n_img, chi), RDTYPE).at[:, 0].set(1.0)
    Lst = jnp.zeros((N, n_img, chi), RDTYPE).at[0].set(e0)
    As, Rs = list(As), [e0]                                                    # Rs[k] = environment of sites >= N-k
    for j in range(N - 1, 0, -1):                                              # right environments, rescaled to unit mean norm
        R = right_env_step(Rs[-1], As[j], phiT[j])
        kappa = jnp.sqrt(jnp.mean(jnp.sum(R ** 2, axis=1)))
        As[j] = As[j] / kappa
        Rs.append(R / kappa)
    Rst = jnp.stack([jnp.zeros((n_img, chi), RDTYPE)] + Rs[::-1])             # Rst[j], j = 0..N (Rst[0] unused)
    return jnp.stack(As), C, Lst, Rst


@partial(jax.jit, static_argnames=("n_steps",))
def half_sweep_right(As, C, Lst, Rst, phiT, Y, n_steps):
    """Bonds j = 0 .. N-2, centre (with label) moving from site 0 to site N-1.

    per bond: B = C A_{j+1};  gradient steps (local_update);  split;  new left environment;  gauge rescaling of the
    environment and the tensors by its root-mean-square norm (the model is unchanged)
    """
    N, chi = As.shape[0], As.shape[1]

    def body(carry, j):
        As, C, Lst = carry
        Bt = jnp.einsum("aslm,mtb->astlb", C, As[j + 1])
        Bt, cost = local_update(Bt, projected_inputs(Lst[j], phiT[j], phiT[j + 1], Rst[j + 2]), Y, n_steps)
        A, C, eps = split_moving_right(Bt, chi)
        L = left_env_step(Lst[j], A, phiT[j])
        kappa = jnp.sqrt(jnp.mean(jnp.sum(L ** 2, axis=1)))
        return (As.at[j].set(A / kappa), C * kappa, Lst.at[j + 1].set(L / kappa)), (cost, eps)
    (As, C, Lst), (cost, eps) = lax.scan(body, (As, C, Lst), jnp.arange(N - 1))
    return As, C, Lst, cost, eps


@partial(jax.jit, static_argnames=("n_steps",))
def half_sweep_left(As, C, Lst, Rst, phiT, Y, n_steps):
    """Bonds j = N-2 .. 0, centre moving from site N-1 back to site 0 (bond tensor B = A_j C, label on site j+1)."""
    N, chi = As.shape[0], As.shape[1]

    def body(carry, j):
        As, C, Rst = carry
        Bt = jnp.einsum("asm,mtlb->astlb", As[j], C)
        Bt, cost = local_update(Bt, projected_inputs(Lst[j], phiT[j], phiT[j + 1], Rst[j + 2]), Y, n_steps)
        C, B, eps = split_moving_left(Bt, chi)
        R = right_env_step(Rst[j + 2], B, phiT[j + 1])
        kappa = jnp.sqrt(jnp.mean(jnp.sum(R ** 2, axis=1)))
        return (As.at[j + 1].set(B / kappa), C * kappa, Rst.at[j + 1].set(R / kappa)), (cost, eps)
    (As, C, Rst), (cost, eps) = lax.scan(body, (As, C, Rst), jnp.arange(N - 2, -1, -1))
    return As, C, Rst, cost, eps


@jax.jit
def sweep_scores(As, C, phi):
    """Scores F[n, l] of snapshots with feature vectors phi (M, N, 2) for the sweep MPS with the label tensor on site 0."""
    n_img, chi = phi.shape[0], As.shape[1]
    R = jnp.zeros((n_img, chi), RDTYPE).at[:, 0].set(1.0)
    R, _ = lax.scan(lambda R, j: (right_env_step(R, As[j], phi[:, j]), None), R, jnp.arange(As.shape[0] - 1, 0, -1))
    return jnp.einsum("ns,slb,nb->nl", phi[:, 0], C[0], R)               # C[0]: left boundary row e0

3. The data: snapshots of Ising ground states

3.1 The model and the sign of its ground state

We use the transverse-field Ising chain with open ends and \(J=1\),

\[H=-J\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}Z_iZ_{i+1}-h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}X_i. \tag{2}\]

For \(h<J\) the ground state is ferromagnetically ordered along \(Z\), for \(h>J\) it is paramagnetic, and the infinite chain has its critical point at \(h_c=J\). The critical point and the long-range order below it follow from the exact free-fermion solution introduced in [6]. The correlation-length exponent \(\nu=1\), which follows from the gap of that solution, and the exact energy of the open chain for every \(N\), used below as a check, are derived in notebook 47, quantum phase transitions (Section 2.3). The parity \(\Pi=\prod_jX_j\) commutes with \(H\), and the ground state of a finite chain is a parity eigenstate with \(\Pi=+1\); in the ordered phase it is a cat-like superposition of the two ferromagnetic directions, with \(\langle Z_j\rangle=0\).

In the \(Z\) basis the matrix elements of \(H\) are

\[\langle\mathbf s\vert H\vert\mathbf s\rangle=-J\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}(-1)^{s_i+s_{i+1}},\qquad \langle\mathbf s'\vert H\vert\mathbf s\rangle=\begin{cases}-h&\text{if }\mathbf s'\text{ and }\mathbf s\text{ differ in one bit,}\\0&\text{otherwise }(\mathbf s'\neq\mathbf s).\end{cases} \tag{3}\]

All off-diagonal elements are \(\leq0\). A Hamiltonian with this property in a given basis is called stoquastic in that basis, a term introduced in [7]. The ground state of such a matrix can be chosen with non-negative amplitudes. For any normalised \(\psi\), the vector \(\vert\psi\vert\) with the amplitudes \(\vert\psi(\mathbf s)\vert\) has

\[\langle\,\vert\psi\vert\,\vert H\vert\,\vert\psi\vert\,\rangle=\sum_{\mathbf s}H_{\mathbf s\mathbf s}\vert\psi(\mathbf s)\vert^2 -\sum_{\mathbf s'\neq\mathbf s}\vert H_{\mathbf s'\mathbf s}\vert\,\vert\psi(\mathbf s')\vert\,\vert\psi(\mathbf s)\vert \leq\langle\psi\vert H\vert\psi\rangle, \tag{4}\]

because the diagonal sums agree and every off-diagonal term of \(\langle\psi\vert H\vert\psi\rangle\), \(H_{\mathbf s'\mathbf s}\psi(\mathbf s')^*\psi(\mathbf s)\), summed with its partner \((\mathbf s\leftrightarrow\mathbf s')\), has a real part of at least \(-2\vert H_{\mathbf s'\mathbf s}\vert\,\vert\psi(\mathbf s')\psi(\mathbf s)\vert\). So \(\vert\psi_0\vert\) is a ground state whenever \(\psi_0\) is: a ground state with non-negative amplitudes always exists. The state sampled below is the parity-even ground state (Section 3.2). Flipping every bit maps \(\mathbf s\) to \(\bar{\mathbf s}\), and \(\Pi\psi_0=\psi_0\) means \(\psi_0(\bar{\mathbf s})=\psi_0(\mathbf s)\), a property that \(\vert\psi_0\vert\) shares; Eq. (4) applied inside the even sector therefore makes \(\vert\psi_0\vert\) an even ground state as well. If the lowest level of the even sector is not degenerate, \(\psi_0\) and \(\vert\psi_0\vert\) are the same state up to a global phase, which is all that Eq. (5) below needs. The gap inside the even sector is known at every \(N\) from the free-fermion solution: the lowest even excitation carries two quasi-particles, of energies \(2s_1\) and \(2s_2\), so the gap is \(2(s_1+s_2)\), with \(s_1\leq s_2\) the two smallest singular values of the bidiagonal matrix of the exact energy (the quasi-particle energies \(2s_n\) are derived in notebook 47, quantum phase transitions, Section 2.3). Its smallest value over the field scan is printed for \(N=16\), \(32\) and \(64\) in Section 3.4, and at \(N=16\) it is reproduced by exact state vectors in Section 3.5, together with the smallest amplitude, which is moreover positive at every field (as at \(N=12\) in the next cell). Hence

\[\psi_0(\mathbf s)=\sqrt{p(\mathbf s)}\qquad\text{for every }\mathbf s, \tag{5}\]

up to one global phase: the snapshot distribution in the \(Z\) basis determines the ground state completely.

The same holds in the \(X\) basis. There the Hamiltonian reads \(U_{\rm H}^{\otimes N}HU_{\rm H}^{\otimes N}=-J\sum_iX_iX_{i+1}-h\sum_iZ_i\), whose off-diagonal elements, \(-J\) between strings that differ in two neighbouring bits, are again \(\leq0\). Flipping a neighbouring pair keeps the parity of the number of ones, which in this basis is the eigenvalue of \(\Pi\). The rotated even ground state is zero on the strings with an odd number of ones, Eq. (4) makes it non-negative on the others, and its smallest amplitude on the even strings, printed at \(N=12\) and \(N=16\), is positive. Every \(X\) snapshot of the ground state therefore contains an even number of ones.

3.2 Ground states by DMRG and a parity projection

The ground states are computed by DMRG, introduced in [8], in its two-site form, with the MPO xxz_mpo(N, 0, 0, -J, hx=-h) of Eq. (2). In the ordered phase the two lowest levels are split by an amount exponentially small in \(N\), and DMRG can return a symmetry-broken combination of them, as found in notebook 47, quantum phase transitions (Section 9.2); here it did so deep in the ordered phase, at the fields printed in Section 3.4. The snapshots of such a state would all point in one direction, and its amplitudes need not be positive. We therefore apply the projector \(\frac12(1+\Pi)\), which maps the broken state onto the parity-even ground state. On an MPS, \(\Pi\) is a product of single-site gates, so \(\Pi\vert\psi\rangle\) is the MPS with every tensor replaced by \(\sum_tX_{st}A^{[j]t}\), and the sum of two MPS is an MPS with block-diagonal tensors,

\[\big(1+\Pi\big)\vert\psi\rangle:\qquad C^{[j]s}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{[j]s}&0\\0&A^{[j]\bar s}\end{pmatrix} \ (0<j<N-1),\qquad C^{[0]s}=\big(A^{[0]s}\ \ A^{[0]\bar s}\big),\qquad C^{[N-1]s}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{[N-1]s}\\A^{[N-1]\bar s}\end{pmatrix}, \tag{6}\]

with \(\bar s=1-s\) (applying \(X\) to a site exchanges its two physical components). The bond dimension doubles. The engine’s mps_recanonicalise brings the result into right-canonical form with the norm on site 0, where it is divided out. Since \(\Vert(1+\Pi)\psi\Vert^2=2(1+\langle\Pi\rangle)\), the norm before the division also tells how broken the DMRG state was: \(2\) for a parity-even state, \(\sqrt2\) for a fully broken one, and \(0\) for a parity-odd one.

3.3 Snapshots by exact sampling

A right-canonical MPS with its norm on site 0 is sampled exactly, site by site, with the conditional probabilities of notebook 57, the MPS Born machine, Eq. (13) there; for complex tensors the squares become \(\vert\cdot\vert^2\). A snapshot in the \(X\) basis is a snapshot of \(U_{\rm H}^{\otimes N}\vert\psi\rangle\), the MPS with the tensors \(\sum_t(U_{\rm H})_{st}A^{[j]t}\) (einsum "st,jatb->jasb"). A unitary acting on the physical leg alone keeps a tensor right-canonical, so the same sampler applies. The function log_amplitude contracts an MPS with a list of strings and returns \(\ln\vert\psi(\mathbf s)\vert\), with the row vectors rescaled to unit norm at every site and the logarithms of the norms accumulated. It works for the complex tensors of DMRG; the reused log_psi does the same for the real tensors of the Born machine.

The checkpoints are independent of the MPS code:

  1. at \(N=12\), the DMRG ground state after the projection against the lowest eigenvector of the sparse \(2^{12}\times2^{12}\) Hamiltonian (scipy eigsh): energy, fidelity, and the signs of all amplitudes in the \(Z\) basis and in the \(X\) basis;
  2. the sampler against the exact distribution of the same 12-site state at \(h=1\), in both bases, by the total variation distance \(\mathrm{TV}=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf s}\vert m(\mathbf s)/M-p(\mathbf s)\vert\) (\(m(\mathbf s)\) the number of times \(\mathbf s\) was drawn) compared with its spread under 200 exact multinomial draws of the same size.

# STEP 1: the model, DMRG + parity projection, the sampler in two bases

HAD = np.array([[1.0, 1.0], [1.0, -1.0]]) / np.sqrt(2)          # Hadamard gate: Z basis -> X basis


def tfim_mpo(N, h, J=1.0):
    """MPO of Eq. (2), H = -J sum Z_i Z_i+1 - h sum X_i (the engine's XXZ MPO with Jxx = Jyy = 0)."""
    return xxz_mpo(N, 0.0, 0.0, -J, hx=-h)


def tfim_exact_energy(N, h, J=1.0):
    """Exact ground-state energy of the open chain: E_0 = -sum_n s_n, s_n the singular values of the N x N
    bidiagonal matrix with h on the diagonal and J above it (free-fermion solution of the open chain)."""
    return -np.sum(np.linalg.svd(h * np.eye(N) + J * np.eye(N, k=1), compute_uv=False))


def parity_project(B):
    """(1 + Pi)|psi> / ||(1 + Pi)|psi>||, Pi = prod_j X_j, Eq. (6).

    MATH            C^{[j] s} = blockdiag(A^{[j] s}, A^{[j] 1-s});  first site: row (A^s, A^{1-s});  last: column
    IMPLEMENTATION  NumPy assembly in storage 2 chi, then mps_recanonicalise (right-canonical, norm on site 0)
    RETURNS         B (N, 2 chi, 2, 2 chi), lam (N+1, 2 chi), ||(1 + Pi) psi|| = sqrt(2 (1 + <Pi>))
    """
    B = np.asarray(B)
    N, chi = B.shape[0], B.shape[1]
    XB = B[:, :, ::-1, :]                                     # X on every site: swap the physical components
    C = np.zeros((N, 2 * chi, 2, 2 * chi), dtype=B.dtype)
    C[:, :chi, :, :chi], C[:, chi:, :, chi:] = B, XB
    C[0] = 0.0
    C[0, 0, :, :chi], C[0, 0, :, chi:] = B[0, 0], XB[0, 0]                     # left boundary: one row
    C[N - 1] = 0.0
    C[N - 1, :chi, :, 0], C[N - 1, chi:, :, 0] = B[N - 1, :, :, 0], XB[N - 1, :, :, 0]   # right boundary: one column
    Bp, lam = mps_recanonicalise(jnp.asarray(C, dtype=CDTYPE))
    nrm = float(jnp.linalg.norm(Bp[0]))
    return Bp.at[0].set(Bp[0] / nrm), lam.at[1:N].divide(nrm), nrm                 # Schmidt values of the normalised state


def ground_state(N, h, chi=16, n_sweeps=4, start=None):
    """DMRG ground state of Eq. (2), projected onto P = +1.

    start      MPS to start from (warm start, e.g. the DMRG state of a neighbouring field); default |+...+>
    RETURNS    dict: B, lam (projected state), energy <H>, ||(1 + Pi) psi_DMRG||, and the raw DMRG state (for the next
               warm start)
    """
    M0 = product_mps("+" * N, chi)[0] if start is None else start
    B, _, _ = dmrg(tfim_mpo(N, h), M0, chi, n_sweeps)
    Bp, lam, nrm = parity_project(B)
    return dict(B=Bp, lam=lam, E=float(mpo_expectation(Bp, tfim_mpo(N, h))), norm_1P=nrm, raw=B)


def field_scan(N, fields, chi=16, n_sweeps=2):
    """Ground states for all fields, from the largest field down, each DMRG warm-started from the previous one.

    SAFETY  a DMRG state mostly in the odd sector (||(1 + Pi) psi|| < 1) carries almost no information on the even
            ground state; that field is recomputed from |+...+> with four sweeps
    """
    out, start = {}, None
    for h in sorted(fields, reverse=True):
        gs = ground_state(N, h, chi, n_sweeps, start)
        restarted = gs["norm_1P"] < 1.0
        if restarted:
            gs = ground_state(N, h, chi, 4)
        out[h], start = dict(gs, restarted=restarted), gs["raw"]
    return out


@jax.jit
def sample_mps(u, B):
    """Exact samples of |psi(s)|^2 for a right-canonical MPS (real or complex) with its norm on site 0.

    u (N, M): uniform random numbers.  RETURNS bits (M, N)
    MATH            exact sampler of the MPS Born machine:  P(s_k = 1 | s_0..s_k-1) = |x A^1|^2 / (|x A^0|^2 + |x A^1|^2)
    JAX             lax.scan over the sites, carry x (M, chi) renormalised per sample;  COST O(N chi^2) per sample
    """
    x0 = jnp.zeros((u.shape[1], B.shape[1]), B.dtype).at[:, 0].set(1.0)

    def body(x, inp):
        A, u_k = inp
        y = jnp.einsum("na,asb->nsb", x, A)
        w = jnp.sum(jnp.abs(y) ** 2, axis=2)
        bit = (u_k < w[:, 1] / (w[:, 0] + w[:, 1])).astype(jnp.int32)
        x = jnp.take_along_axis(y, bit[:, None, None], axis=1)[:, 0]
        return x / jnp.linalg.norm(x, axis=1, keepdims=True), bit
    _, bits = lax.scan(body, x0, (B, u))
    return bits.T


def rotate_physical(B, U):
    """Tensors sum_t U[s, t] A^{[j] t}: the MPS of U^{(x) N} |psi> (still right-canonical for unitary U)."""
    return jnp.einsum("st,jatb->jasb", jnp.asarray(U, dtype=B.dtype), B)


def snapshots(seed, B, M, basis="z"):
    """M exact snapshots of the MPS B in the Z or the X basis (fixed seed).  RETURNS int array (M, N)."""
    Bb = B if basis == "z" else rotate_physical(B, HAD)
    u = jnp.asarray(np.random.default_rng(seed).random((B.shape[0], M)), dtype=RDTYPE)
    return np.asarray(sample_mps(u, Bb))


@jax.jit
def log_amplitude(B, v):
    """ln |psi(s)| for strings v (M, N) of any (real or complex) padded MPS, rescaled per site."""
    oh = jnp.swapaxes(jax.nn.one_hot(v, 2, dtype=B.dtype), 0, 1)
    L0 = jnp.zeros((v.shape[0], B.shape[1]), B.dtype).at[:, 0].set(1.0)

    def body(carry, inp):
        L, lz = carry
        A, o = inp
        L = jnp.einsum("na,ns,asb->nb", L, o, A)
        n = jnp.linalg.norm(L, axis=1)
        return (L / n[:, None], lz + jnp.log(n)), None
    (L, lz), _ = lax.scan(body, (L0, jnp.zeros(v.shape[0], RDTYPE)), (B, oh))
    return lz + jnp.log(jnp.abs(L[:, 0]))


def tfim_sparse(N, h, J=1.0):
    """Sparse 2^N x 2^N matrix of Eq. (2), built from Kronecker products (validation only, small N)."""
    Xs, Zs, I = sparse.csr_matrix([[0.0, 1.0], [1.0, 0.0]]), sparse.csr_matrix([[1.0, 0.0], [0.0, -1.0]]), sparse.identity(2)

    def op(ops):
        m = sparse.identity(1, format="csr")
        for k in range(N):
            m = sparse.kron(m, ops.get(k, I), format="csr")
        return m
    return (-J * sum(op({i: Zs, i + 1: Zs}) for i in range(N - 1)) - h * sum(op({i: Xs}) for i in range(N))).tocsr()


def phase_fixed(psi):
    """Remove the global phase: divide by the phase of the largest amplitude."""
    k = np.argmax(np.abs(psi))
    return psi * np.exp(-1j * np.angle(psi[k]))

3.4 The data sets

The ground states are computed for three chain lengths, \(N=16\), \(32\) and \(64\), at 18 fields between \(0.2\) and \(1.8\), denser near \(h_c=1\). 3.5 Exact state vectors at \(N=16\)

At \(N=16\) the full state vector has \(2^{16}=65536\) amplitudes, few enough to compute every ground state of the scan exactly and compare it with DMRG one to one. The exact ground states come from restarted Lanczos iterations on the state vector, run separately in the two parity sectors with the projector \(\frac12(1\pm\Pi)\) applied after every matrix-vector product, as derived in notebook 47, quantum phase transitions (Section 3). The product \(H\vec v\) is matrix-free: the diagonal of Eq. (3) is one stored vector, and each field term \(-hX_j\) reads the amplitude of the string with bit \(j\) flipped, an index computed once as an exclusive OR with \(2^{N-1-j}\). Three runs per field give the even ground state \(E_0^+\), the odd ground state \(E_0^-\), and the second even level \(E_1^+\) (Lanczos in the even sector with the ground state projected out). The gap of the chain is \(E_0^--E_0^+\) (the lowest excitation is a single quasi-particle and changes the parity), and \(E_1^+-E_0^+\) is the gap inside the even sector, which Section 3.1 needs to be non-zero. Both are compared with the free-fermion values, \(2s_{\min}\) and \(2(s_1+s_2)\) with \(s_1\leq s_2\) the two smallest singular values of the bidiagonal matrix of the exact energy. For every field the cell then contracts the projected DMRG state into a vector of \(2^{16}\) amplitudes and prints, side by side: the energy difference, the infidelity \(1-\vert\langle\psi_{\rm MPS}\vert\psi_{\rm ED}\rangle\vert^2\), the largest deviations of all \(\langle Z_iZ_j\rangle\) and \(\langle X_j\rangle\) and of the string \(\langle\prod_{k=4}^{11}X_k\rangle\), the total variation distance between the exact snapshot distributions of the two vectors in both bases, and, from the exact vector, the smallest amplitude in the \(Z\) basis, the smallest amplitude on the even strings in the \(X\) basis, and \(\langle\Pi\rangle\).

def tfim_matvec(v, h, J=1.0):
    """H v for a flat real vector v of 2^N amplitudes, Eq. (3), matrix-free.

    MATH            (H v)(s) = -J sum_i (-1)^(s_i + s_i+1) v(s) - h sum_j v(s^(j))
    COST            N + 1 passes over the 2^N amplitudes
    """
    return -J * ZZ_DIAG * v - h * sum(v[f] for f in FLIP)


def sector_lanczos(h, sign, deflate=None, m=60, restarts=3, seed=0):
    """Lowest eigenpair of H inside the parity sector `sign` (+1 even, -1 odd) by restarted Lanczos.

    MATH            matvec v -> Q (1 + sign Pi)/2 H v, Pi v (s) = v(s with every bit flipped), Q = 1 - |d><d| with
                    d = deflate (a converged lower state of the same sector) or Q = 1
    IMPLEMENTATION  the engine's `lanczos` builds the Krylov basis; the lowest eigenvector of the small tridiagonal
                    matrix, mapped back, starts the next run
    RETURNS         E, normalised real vector, residual |H v - E v|
    """
    def mv(v):
        w = tfim_matvec(np.asarray(v), h)
        w = 0.5 * (w + sign * w[FLIP_ALL])
        if deflate is not None:
            w = w - deflate * (deflate @ w)
        return jnp.asarray(w)
    v = np.random.default_rng(seed).standard_normal(D_X)
    v = 0.5 * (v + sign * v[FLIP_ALL])
    if deflate is not None:
        v = v - deflate * (deflate @ v)
    for _ in range(restarts):
        a, b, V = lanczos(mv, jnp.asarray(v / np.linalg.norm(v)), m)
        w, S = np.linalg.eigh(np.diag(a) + np.diag(b, 1) + np.diag(b, -1))
        v = np.array(jnp.tensordot(jnp.asarray(S[:, 0]), V, axes=1)).real
        v = v / np.linalg.norm(v)
    return float(w[0]), v, float(np.linalg.norm(np.asarray(mv(v)) - w[0] * v))


def hadamard_all(v):
    """Amplitudes in the X basis: the Hadamard gate applied to every qubit of the flat state vector v."""
    t = v.reshape((2,) * N_X)
    for q in range(N_X):
        t = np.moveaxis(np.tensordot(HAD, np.moveaxis(t, q, 0), axes=1), 0, q)
    return t.reshape(-1)


def dense_observables(v):

4. Part A: quantum state tomography with a prior

4.1 What one basis can determine

One basis reveals only the \(2^N\) diagonal elements of a density matrix in that basis, so without a prior it can never determine the state. The prior of Section 1.2 replaces the density matrix by a pure state with non-negative amplitudes, for which Eq. (5) turns the diagonal into the whole state, and replaces the \(2^N\) amplitudes by an MPS with \(O(N\chi^2)\) numbers. The reconstruction then has three sources of error: the finite number \(M\) of snapshots (statistical error), the bond dimension (a truncation error, present even for \(M\to\infty\)), and the training (a model that has not found the best MPS). If the prior is wrong, a fourth error appears that no amount of data removes (Section 4.9).

4.2 The estimator

The MPS Born machine is trained on the \(Z\) snapshots by minimising the negative log-likelihood (NLL), \(\mathcal L=-\frac1M\sum_m\ln P(\mathbf s_m)\), with the two-site sweeps derived in notebook 57, the MPS Born machine (Section 4). It returns a normalised distribution \(P(\mathbf s)=\phi(\mathbf s)^2\) with a real MPS \(\phi\). The reconstructed state is defined by the prior,

\[\psi_{\rm rec}(\mathbf s)=\sqrt{P(\mathbf s)}=\vert\phi(\mathbf s)\vert. \tag{7}\]

The likelihood depends only on \(\phi^2\) and does not control the signs of \(\phi\). We start the training from an MPS whose amplitudes are all positive: its tensors are drawn with positive entries and then brought to canonical form, which changes only the gauge. The amplitude of a string that occurs in the training set rarely changes its sign: the cost \(-\ln\phi(\mathbf s)^2\) diverges where the amplitude vanishes, so a step of the line search lands near that point only if it jumps across it, which the discrete grid of step sizes and the truncation after each SVD occasionally allow. The amplitudes of strings that never occurred are not protected at all, and \(\phi\) may acquire negative amplitudes there. Eq. (7) removes them. The price is that \(\psi_{\rm rec}\) is no longer an MPS, so its observables are computed by sampling (Section 4.4).

The training uses the settings of the Born machine: one line-search step per bond, a relative SVD cutoff of \(10^{-7}\), an initial MPS of bond dimension 2 with positive amplitudes, tensors stored in arrays of width 8 (enough for every cap used here), and at most 8 loops, a loop being one sweep to the right and one back. The number of loops and the maximal bond dimension \(\chi_{\max}\) are chosen on a validation set of \(M/4\) further snapshots that are not used in the training. After every loop the NLL of the validation set is computed and the loop with the lowest value is kept (early stopping); among several caps \(\chi_{\max}\) the one with the lowest validation NLL is selected. The validation NLL estimates the cross entropy \(-\sum_{\mathbf s}p\ln P=S(p)+D_{\rm KL}(p\Vert P)\), with \(S(p)=-\sum_{\mathbf s}p\ln p\) the Shannon entropy, introduced in [9], of the snapshot distribution (derived in notebook 57, the MPS Born machine, Eq. (6)). \(S(p)\) does not depend on the model, so among the candidate models the one with the lowest validation NLL is the one with the smallest divergence from the truth, up to the statistical error of the validation set.

4.3 Kullback–Leibler divergence and fidelity

Since the true state is known here, the reconstruction can be graded. Let \(p\) be the true distribution and \(\psi=\sqrt p\) the true state. The Kullback–Leibler divergence of the model from the truth and the fidelity of the reconstruction are means over the true distribution,

\[D_{\rm KL}(p\Vert P)=\mathbb E_{\mathbf s\sim p}\Big[\ln\frac{p(\mathbf s)}{P(\mathbf s)}\Big],\qquad F=\vert\langle\psi\vert\psi_{\rm rec}\rangle\vert^2=\Big(\sum_{\mathbf s}\sqrt{p(\mathbf s)P(\mathbf s)}\Big)^2 =\Big(\mathbb E_{\mathbf s\sim p}\sqrt{\frac{P(\mathbf s)}{p(\mathbf s)}}\Big)^2. \tag{8}\]

Both are estimated from \(20000\) exact test snapshots of the true state, with \(\ln p(\mathbf s)\) of every test snapshot from the DMRG MPS and \(\ln P(\mathbf s)\) from the model. Their statistical errors are standard errors of the mean; for \(F=b^2\), with \(b\) the mean inside the bracket, the error is \(2b\) times the standard error of \(b\).

The two quantities are connected by one fact: the geometric mean of a positive random quantity \(Y\) never exceeds its ordinary mean, \(e^{\mathbb E[\ln Y]}\leq\mathbb E[Y]\), with equality only when \(Y\) does not vary. For example, \(Y=1\) or \(Y=100\) with equal probability has the geometric mean \(\sqrt{1\cdot100}=10\) and the ordinary mean \((1+100)/2=50.5\). With \(Y=\sqrt{P/p}\) and \(\mathbf s\sim p\), Eq. (8) gives \(\mathbb E_p[Y]=\sqrt F\), and \(\mathbb E_p[\ln Y]=-\frac12D_{\rm KL}(p\Vert P)\), so

\[\sqrt F\geq e^{-D_{\rm KL}(p\Vert P)/2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F\geq e^{-D_{\rm KL}(p\Vert P)}. \tag{9}\]

A small divergence therefore guarantees a high fidelity. For the MPS \(\phi\) itself, the overlap \(\vert\langle\psi\vert\phi\rangle\vert^2=\big(\sum_{\mathbf s}\sqrt{p(\mathbf s)}\,\phi(\mathbf s)\big)^2\) is computed exactly by mps_overlap. It is at most the exact \(F\), with equality when \(\phi\geq0\) wherever \(p>0\), so the difference measures the weight lost to negative amplitudes of \(\phi\). The sampled estimate of \(F\) carries its statistical error, so in a printed table the exact overlap may also lie slightly above it.

4.4 Observables of the reconstruction: local estimators

Diagonal observables, such as \(\langle Z_iZ_j\rangle\), are means of \((-1)^{s_i+s_j}\) over samples of \(P\), drawn exactly from the trained MPS with the sampler of Section 3.3. Off-diagonal ones are means over \(P\) as well, for a positive wave function. With \(\mathbf s^{(j)}\) the string \(\mathbf s\) with bit \(j\) flipped, \(X_j\vert\mathbf s\rangle=\vert\mathbf s^{(j)}\rangle\), and for real non-negative \(\psi_{\rm rec}\),

\[\langle X_j\rangle=\sum_{\mathbf s}\psi_{\rm rec}(\mathbf s)\,\psi_{\rm rec}(\mathbf s^{(j)}) =\sum_{\mathbf s}P(\mathbf s)\sqrt{\frac{P(\mathbf s^{(j)})}{P(\mathbf s)}} =\mathbb E_{\mathbf s\sim P}\sqrt{\frac{P(\mathbf s^{(j)})}{P(\mathbf s)}}. \tag{10}\]

With Eq. (2), the energy is the mean of the local energy

\[E_{\rm loc}(\mathbf s)=\frac{\langle\mathbf s\vert H\vert\psi_{\rm rec}\rangle}{\psi_{\rm rec}(\mathbf s)} =-J\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}(-1)^{s_i+s_{i+1}}-h\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\sqrt{\frac{P(\mathbf s^{(j)})}{P(\mathbf s)}}, \tag{11}\]

If \(\psi_{\rm rec}\) is an exact eigenstate, \(H\psi_{\rm rec}=E\psi_{\rm rec}\), then \(E_{\rm loc}(\mathbf s)=E\) for every string: the estimate has zero variance, which makes the spread of \(E_{\rm loc}\) a sensitive test of a ground state. Each sample needs \(N\) further amplitudes, one per flipped bit, at \(O(N\chi^2)\) each as implemented here; with the partial products of the chain from the left and from the right cached, all \(N\) of them together cost \(O(N\chi^2)\).

These estimates need only \(Z\) snapshots and the prior. Without the prior, \(\langle X_j\rangle\) must be measured in the \(X\) basis, and the direct estimate of the energy takes two data sets, \(Z\) snapshots for the bond terms and \(X\) snapshots for the field term. It is the baseline of Section 4.7.

def init_positive_mps(seed, N, chi_s, chi_init=2):
    """Random MPS with positive entries on a chi_init block (all amplitudes > 0), right-canonical, centre on site 0.

    MATH  as the initial MPS of the Born machine, with entries uniform in [0.5, 1.5) instead of Gaussian;
          the SVD sweep changes only the gauge, so the state and its positive amplitudes are unchanged
    """
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    blk = (np.arange(chi_s) < chi_init).astype(float)
    A = (0.5 + rng.random((N, chi_s, 2, chi_s))) * blk[None, :, None, None] * blk[None, None, None, :]
    A[0] *= (np.arange(chi_s) == 0)[:, None, None]
    A[N - 1] *= (np.arange(chi_s) == 0)[None, None, :]
    for j in range(N - 1, 0, -1):
        U, lam, Vt = np.linalg.svd(np.einsum("asm,mtb->astb", A[j - 1], A[j]).reshape(2 * chi_s, 2 * chi_s),
                                   full_matrices=False)
        keep = ((lam > 1e-14 * lam[0]) & (np.arange(lam.size) < chi_s))[:chi_s]
        A[j] = (Vt[:chi_s] * keep[:, None]).reshape(chi_s, 2, chi_s)
        C = (U[:, :chi_s] * (lam[:chi_s] * keep)[None, :]).reshape(chi_s, 2, chi_s)
        A[j - 1] = C / np.linalg.norm(C)
    return jnp.asarray(A, dtype=RDTYPE)

    R_init, lnR_init = initial_right_envs(As, oh)
    Rst = jnp.concatenate([jnp.zeros_like(e0), R_init, e0])
    lnR = jnp.concatenate([jnp.zeros((1, n), RDTYPE), lnR_init, jnp.zeros((1, n), RDTYPE)])
    Lst = jnp.zeros((N, n, chi_s), RDTYPE).at[0].set(e0[0])
    lnL = jnp.zeros((N, n), RDTYPE)
    best, val_hist = (np.inf, None, 0), []
    for loop in range(max_loops):
        As, Lst, lnL, Rst, lnR, _ = training_loop(As, Lst, lnL, Rst, lnR, oh, chi_max, 1e-7, 1)
        val_hist.append(nll_of(As, v_val)[0])
        if val_hist[-1] < best[0]:
            best = (val_hist[-1], As, loop + 1)
    return best[1], best[2], np.array(val_hist)


def select_and_train(v, v_val, chis=(2, 4, 8), **kw):
    """train_born for every cap in chis; keep the model with the lowest validation NLL.

    RETURNS  As, chi_max, best loop, {chi_max: validation NLL}, validation NLL after every loop of the selected model
    """
    runs = {chi: train_born(v, v_val, chi, **kw) for chi in chis}
    table = {chi: float(r[2][r[1] - 1]) for chi, r in runs.items()}
    chi_best = min(table, key=table.get)
    return runs[chi_best][0], chi_best, runs[chi_best][1], table, runs[chi_best][2]


def fidelity_kl(As, v_test, lnp_test, B_true=None):
    """Eq. (8) on test snapshots of the true state.

    ARGUMENTS  v_test (M, N) exact samples of p;  lnp_test = ln p(v_test) of the true state
    RETURNS    dict: KL and its standard error, F = BC^2 and its standard error, and (if B_true is given) the exact
               |<psi|phi>|^2 of the MPS phi itself
    """
    lnP = 2 * np.asarray(log_psi(As, jnp.asarray(v_test))) - float(log_Z_transfer(As))
    d = lnp_test - lnP
    kl, kl_se = mean_se(d)
    r = np.exp(-0.5 * d)                                     # sqrt(P / p)
    bc, bc_se = mean_se(r)
    out = dict(kl=kl, kl_se=kl_se, F=bc ** 2, F_se=2 * bc * bc_se)
    if B_true is not None:
        out["F_mps"] = abs(complex(mps_overlap(jnp.asarray(As, dtype=CDTYPE), B_true))) ** 2
    return out


def flips(v):
    """All single-bit flips of every string: v (M, N) -> (M, N, N) with entry [m, j] = v_m with bit j flipped."""
    N = v.shape[1]
    return np.abs(v[:, None, :] - np.eye(N, dtype=v.dtype)[None])


def local_observables(log_amp_fn, v, h, J=1.0):
    """Eq. (10) and the energy from strings v (M, N) sampled from |psi|^2 of a POSITIVE wave function.

    log_amp_fn(strings) -> ln |psi| (any normalisation: only ratios enter)
    RETURNS  dict of (mean, standard error): X (per site), ZZ (per bond), energy
    """
    M, N = v.shape
    la = np.asarray(log_amp_fn(v))
    la_f = np.asarray(log_amp_fn(flips(v).reshape(-1, N))).reshape(M, N)
    ratio = np.exp(la_f - la[:, None])                         # psi(s^(j)) / psi(s)
    zz = (1 - 2 * v[:, :-1]) * (1 - 2 * v[:, 1:])              # (-1)^(s_i + s_i+1)
    e_loc = -J * zz.sum(axis=1) - h * ratio.sum(axis=1)
    return dict(X=mean_se(ratio), ZZ=mean_se(zz), E=mean_se(e_loc))


def model_samples(seed, As, M):
    """M exact samples of the trained Born machine (right-canonical, centre on site 0)."""
    u = jnp.asarray(np.random.default_rng(seed).random((As.shape[0], M)), dtype=RDTYPE)
    return np.asarray(sample_mps(u, As))

4.6 Fidelity against the number of snapshots and the bond dimension

At the critical field \(h=1\) and \(N=32\) we train on \(M\in\{1000,3000,10000,20000\}\) snapshots (plus \(M/4\) validation snapshots) with \(\chi_{\max}\in\{2,4,8\}\) and grade every model on the same \(20000\) test snapshots. def truncate_mps(B, chi):
    """Truncate a right-canonical MPS (norm on site 0) to bond dimension chi by one left-to-right SVD sweep.

    MATH            centre C_j (a s, b) = U S V^dag;  A_j = U[:, :chi];  C_{j+1} = (S V^dag)[:chi] A_{j+1};  normalise
    RETURNS         padded MPS of the same array shape (NumPy), and the discarded weights of the N-1 bonds
    """
    A = np.array(B)
    n_s, c_s = A.shape[0], A.shape[1]
    eps = []
    for j in range(n_s - 1):
        U, S, Vh = np.linalg.svd(A[j].reshape(2 * c_s, c_s), full_matrices=False)
        eps.append(np.sum(S[chi:] ** 2) / np.sum(S ** 2))
        S = np.where(np.arange(S.size) < chi, S, 0.0)
        A[j] = (U * (S > 0)).reshape(c_s, 2, c_s)
        A[j + 1] = np.einsum("ab,bsc->asc", S[:, None] * Vh, A[j + 1])
    A[-1] /= np.linalg.norm(A[-1])
    return A, np.array(eps)

4.7 Across the transition: fidelity and observables

The same procedure, with \(M=10000\) snapshots and \(\chi_{\max}\in\{2,4\}\) selected on the validation set (\(\chi_{\max}=8\) was never selected in Section 4.6), is applied at five fields. For every reconstruction the cell evaluates, from \(4000\) samples of the model, the energy per site, the transverse magnetisation \(\langle X_{16}\rangle\) in the middle of the chain and the correlator \(\langle Z_8Z_{24}\rangle\), and compares them with the DMRG values and the energy with the exact one. It also prints the lower bound on the energy error that follows from the fidelity: writing \(\psi_{\rm rec}=\sqrt F\,\psi_0+\sqrt{1-F}\,\psi_\perp\) with \(\psi_\perp\) orthogonal to the ground state, \(\langle H\rangle-E_0=(1-F)(\langle\psi_\perp\vert H\vert\psi_\perp\rangle-E_0)\geq(1-F)\Delta\), with \(\Delta=E_1-E_0\) the gap of the open chain. \(E_1\) lies in the odd parity sector, which does not change the bound. The quasi-particle energies of the open chain are twice the singular values of the bidiagonal matrix that gives the exact energy, so \(\Delta\) is twice the smallest one, as derived in notebook 47, quantum phase transitions (Section 2.3). The direct baselines use snapshots: \(\langle Z_8Z_{24}\rangle\) as the mean over the \(10000\) training snapshots, and the energy from \(5000\) of them and \(5000\) new \(X\) snapshots, \(10000\) in all, against the \(Z\) snapshots of the reconstruction, \(10000\) for training and \(M/4\) for validation. At \(h=1\) the training is repeated on three independent data sets to measure the spread of the reconstruction itself.

4.9 Where one basis is not enough: signs and mixed states

Two states produce exactly the same \(Z\) snapshots as the ground state \(\vert\psi\rangle\) at \(h=1\):

  • the signed state \(\vert\psi_\sigma\rangle=\prod_{j\ {\rm odd}}Z_j\vert\psi\rangle\), with amplitudes \(\psi_\sigma(\mathbf s)=\sigma(\mathbf s)\psi(\mathbf s)\), \(\sigma(\mathbf s)=(-1)^{\sum_{j\,{\rm odd}}s_j}\). It is the ground state of \(-J\sum_iZ_iZ_{i+1}-h\sum_j(-1)^jX_j\), whose off-diagonal elements on the odd sites are positive: this Hamiltonian is not stoquastic in the \(Z\) basis;
  • the dephased state \(\rho_{\rm d}=\sum_{\mathbf s}p(\mathbf s)\vert\mathbf s\rangle\langle\mathbf s\vert\), the ground state after complete dephasing in the \(Z\) basis: a classical mixture with the same diagonal.

The reconstruction from \(Z\) snapshots is the same in all three cases, \(\psi_{\rm rec}=\sqrt P\approx\psi\). Its fidelity with the signed state is \(\vert\langle\psi_\sigma\vert\psi_{\rm rec}\rangle\vert^2\), estimated by Eq. (8) with the factor \(\sigma(\mathbf s)\) inside the mean; a perfect reconstruction would give \(\langle\psi\vert\prod_{j\,{\rm odd}}Z_j\vert\psi\rangle^2\), computed exactly from the MPS. Its fidelity with the mixed state is \(\langle\psi_{\rm rec}\vert\rho_{\rm d}\vert\psi_{\rm rec}\rangle=\sum_{\mathbf s}p(\mathbf s)P(\mathbf s)\), the mean of \(P\) over test snapshots.

The \(X\) basis tells the three states apart. Since \(X_j\) flips bit \(j\), and flipping an odd bit changes the sign \(\sigma\), \(\langle\psi_\sigma\vert X_j\vert\psi_\sigma\rangle=(-1)^j\langle\psi\vert X_j\vert\psi\rangle\): the transverse magnetisation alternates in sign. For \(\rho_{\rm d}\), every \(X\)-basis string \(\mathbf x\) has the probability \(\sum_{\mathbf s}p(\mathbf s)\vert\langle\mathbf x\vert\mathbf s\rangle\vert^2=2^{-N}\), because \(\vert\langle\mathbf x\vert\mathbf s\rangle\vert^2=2^{-N}\) for every pair; its \(X\) snapshots are uniformly random bits and every \(\langle X_j\rangle\) vanishes. The reconstruction predicts \(\langle X_j\rangle>0\) on every site (Eq. (10) is a mean of positive numbers), and \(X\) snapshots of either state contradict it.

For the signed state the \(X\) data also repair the reconstruction, within a restricted class. If the true state is \(\prod_jg_j^{s_j}\sqrt{p(\mathbf s)}\) with unknown signs \(g_j=\pm1\) (a positive state rotated by \(Z\) on some sites), then \(\langle X_j\rangle=g_j\langle X_j\rangle_{\psi}\), and the sign of the measured \(\langle X_j\rangle\) is \(g_j\). The cell uses the \(M=10000\), \(\chi_{\max}=4\) reconstruction of Section 4.6, measures \(\langle X_j\rangle\) from \(2000\) \(X\) snapshots of \(\psi_\sigma\) and of \(\rho_{\rm d}\), compares them with the prediction of the reconstruction, reads off the \(g_j\), and computes the fidelity of the corrected reconstruction. Each comparison uses a combined error: the standard error of the \(X\) data, that of the 4000 model samples, and the spread of the reconstruction over independent data sets measured in Section 4.7.

4.10 Towards general MPS tomography

A general state of \(N\) qubits has \(2^N\) signs or phases, and no fixed pair of bases determines them all. MPS tomography, introduced in [3], keeps the cost polynomial in \(N\) by measuring every block of \(k\) neighbouring qubits in a tomographically complete set of local bases, estimating the \(k\)-site reduced density matrices \(\rho_i\), and finding the MPS compatible with them by a singular-value thresholding iteration with DMRG-like steps. It also certifies the result: the estimate \(\vert\hat\psi\rangle\) is the ground state of a parent Hamiltonian \(\sum_i\hat T_i\) of local terms, and a lower bound on the fidelity of the laboratory state with \(\vert\hat\psi\rangle\) follows from the measured \(\mathrm{tr}(\hat T_i\rho_i)\) and the gap of that Hamiltonian, without any assumption on the laboratory state. Giving every \(k\)-th qubit the same Pauli basis measures all blocks at once, so \(3^k\) settings suffice whatever \(N\) is: \(3^2=9\) or \(3^3=27\) for \(k=2\) or \(3\), against \(3^{32}\approx1.9\times10^{15}\) for full tomography of 32 qubits. The scheme was carried out on up to 14 trapped ions in [10], with \(k=3\). Generative models extend the same way: states with phases were reconstructed in [2] by adding snapshots in rotated bases, and the MPS Born machine was trained on snapshots in random local bases, which determine a pure state, in [11]. The likelihood then becomes a sum over bases with a complex MPS, and the sign prior of Eq. (5) is dropped. A matrix product operator does the same for mixed states, with two physical indices per site.

5.1 The classifier and the training windows

Part B reconstructs nothing. Its question is one bit per field, “ordered or disordered?”, and the data are again snapshots. The approach of [5] trains on configurations labelled by their phase over a range of temperatures on both sides of the transition and reads the transition off the crossing of the outputs, there for a lattice the network was not trained on. An MPS classifier trained on \(Z\) and \(X\) snapshots of six central sites of 32-site antiferromagnetic transverse-field Ising ground states, taken far from the transition, was studied in [12]; evaluated on entangled input states, translation-invariant matrix product states of the infinite chain loaded on a trapped-ion quantum computer, it placed the transition at \(h=0.962\) and \(0.994\) (for classifier bond dimensions 2 and 4), the point where the output crosses \(\frac12\), located by a linear fit near the crossing; the test states there describe the infinite chain.

We use the MPS classifier with two labels, \(\ell=0\) (ordered) and \(\ell=1\) (disordered). A snapshot is turned into a product state of one-hot vectors \(\mathbf e(s)=(1-s,\,s)\), so the score of a snapshot is a single component of the weight MPS, \(f^\ell(\mathbf s)=W^\ell_{s_0\cdots s_{N-1}}\), and the softmax of the two scores is the classifier probability \(q(\ell\vert\mathbf s)\). Training uses the sweeping algorithm of notebook 56, tensor networks as machine-learning models (Section 6) with the cross-entropy: two sweeps, bond dimension \(\chi=4\), and at every bond 10 gradient steps with the step size \(1/\Lambda\), where \(\Lambda\) is an upper bound on the curvature of the cost that guarantees that no step increases it (derived there in Section 6.3). The training set has \(500\) snapshots at each of the fields \(h\in\{0.2,0.3,0.4,0.5\}\) (label ordered) and \(h\in\{1.5,1.6,1.7,1.8\}\) (label disordered), all at least \(0.5J\) from \(h_c\). The trained classifier is applied to \(n_{\rm test}=500\) fresh snapshots at each of the 18 fields, and the mean probability of the label “ordered”,

\[\bar q_{\rm ord}(h)=\frac1{n_{\rm test}}\sum_{m=1}^{n_{\rm test}}q\big(\ell=0\,\vert\,\mathbf s_m\big),\qquad\mathbf s_m\sim p_h, \tag{12}\]

with \(p_h\) the snapshot distribution of the ground state at field \(h\) in the measured basis, is recorded. The field \(h^*(N)\) where \(\bar q_{\rm ord}=\frac12\), found by linear interpolation between grid points, is the transition seen by the classifier. Each experiment is repeated with three independent training and test sets; the standard error of \(h^*\) is the standard deviation of the three values divided by \(\sqrt3\), a rough estimate with two degrees of freedom.

5.2 What each basis sees

In the \(Z\) basis a snapshot records the spin configuration: ordered snapshots are long ferromagnetic domains, pointing up or down with equal probability, disordered ones are short-range correlated. The order parameter, the correlator \(\langle Z_{j_1}Z_{j_2}\rangle\) between the distant sites \(j_1=N/4\) and \(j_2=3N/4\), and the squared magnetisation \(\langle m_z^2\rangle\), \(m_z=\frac1N\sum_j(-1)^{s_j}\), are means over \(Z\) snapshots; \(\langle X_j\rangle\) is invisible. In the \(X\) basis the roles swap: disordered snapshots are mostly \(s_j=0\) (the state is close to \(\vert+\cdots+\rangle\)), ordered ones look random. Here the natural quantity is the disorder operator, the string \(\langle\prod_{k=j_1}^{j_2-1}X_k\rangle\), which flips all spins between \(j_1\) and \(j_2\) and so inserts two domain walls; on a snapshot it is the parity \((-1)^{\sum_{k=j_1}^{j_2-1}s_k}\).

The Kramers–Wannier duality explains why the string is the order parameter of the disordered phase. Define dual Pauli operators on the bonds,

\[\mu^x_{j+\frac12}=Z_jZ_{j+1},\qquad\mu^z_{j+\frac12}=\prod_{k\leq j}X_k,\qquad X_j=\mu^z_{j-\frac12}\mu^z_{j+\frac12},\qquad\prod_{k=j_1}^{j_2-1}X_k=\mu^z_{j_1-\frac12}\mu^z_{j_2-\frac12}. \tag{13}\]

They obey the same algebra as Pauli matrices on each bond (\(\mu^x\) and \(\mu^z\) anticommute on the same bond and commute on different bonds), and Eq. (2) becomes \(-J\sum\mu^x-h\sum\mu^z\mu^z\): the same chain with \(J\) and \(h\) exchanged. The string is therefore a two-point function of dual spins; in the bulk it behaves at field \(h\) as the order correlator does at \(J^2/h\), tending to a non-zero constant for \(h>J\) and decaying with the distance for \(h<J\). On the open chain the boundaries of the two descriptions differ, so the correspondence \(h\leftrightarrow J^2/h\) between the two curves (a reflection about \(h_c\) on a logarithmic field axis) is only approximate. Each basis also contains local quantities, the nearest-neighbour correlator \(\langle Z_jZ_{j+1}\rangle\) and the transverse magnetisation \(\langle X_j\rangle\), which change smoothly through \(h_c\).

5.3 Fixed-level crossings and scale-invariant crossings

A classifier crossing \(\bar q_{\rm ord}=\frac12\), and each normalised statistic crossing \(\frac12\), are crossings at a fixed level, set by the training windows. For the order parameter the limit of such a crossing can be computed. In the infinite chain \(\langle Z_0Z_r\rangle\to m^2\) for \(r\to\infty\), with \(m^2=(1-h^2)^{1/4}\) below \(h_c\) [6]; the level set by the windows is crossed where \(m^2(h)\) equals it, a field below \(h_c\). By the duality of Eq. (13) the string tends to \((1-h^{-2})^{1/4}\) above \(h_c\), and its fixed-level crossing lies above \(h_c\). Near the critical point the order correlator has the finite-size scaling form introduced in [13]

\[\langle Z_{j_1}Z_{j_2}\rangle\approx N^{-2\beta/\nu}f\big((h-h_c)N^{1/\nu}\big),\qquad\frac{2\beta}\nu=\frac14,\quad\nu=1, \tag{14}\]

with a scaling function \(f\), the correlation-length exponent \(\nu\) of Section 3.1 and the order-parameter exponent \(\beta=\frac18\), defined by \(m\propto(h_c-h)^\beta\) (consistent with \(m^2=(1-h^2)^{1/4}\)). At \(h_c\) the correlator vanishes as \(N^{-1/4}\), so for large \(N\) a fixed level is reached at a field that tends to the field \(h<h_c\) where \(m^2(h)\) equals the level. A scale-invariant combination removes the prefactor: the curves of \(N^{1/4}\langle Z_{j_1}Z_{j_2}\rangle\) for \(N\) and \(2N\) are both \(f(x)\) with \(x=(h-h_c)N\), and they cross where \(f(x)=f(2x)\), that is at \(x=0\) up to corrections to scaling. The Binder cumulant \(U=1-\langle m_z^4\rangle/(3\langle m_z^2\rangle^2)\), computed from the same \(Z\) snapshots, is dimensionless and crosses for two sizes near \(h_c\) for the same reason. Even two unscaled curves cross near \(h_c\): equating \(N^{-1/4}f(x)\) and \((2N)^{-1/4}f(2x)\) fixes \(x\) at a value independent of \(N\), so the crossing approaches \(h_c\) as \(1/N\). The classifier output \(\bar q_{\rm ord}\) is a mean over snapshots of a function of each snapshot, not a function of \(\langle Z_{j_1}Z_{j_2}\rangle\); the same argument applies to it only under the assumption that this mean obeys a scaling form like Eq. (14), with its own prefactor.

The free-fermion solution of the chain [6], written here for the open chain with Majorana operators, gives all four statistics without sampling. The Jordan–Wigner mapping of the spins to fermions, introduced in [14], writes each spin through two Majorana operators, \(\gamma_{2j}=\big(\prod_{k<j}X_k\big)Z_j\) and \(\gamma_{2j+1}=\big(\prod_{k<j}X_k\big)Y_j\), with \(\gamma_m^2=1\) and \(\gamma_m\gamma_n=-\gamma_n\gamma_m\) for \(m\neq n\). Then \(X_j=i\gamma_{2j}\gamma_{2j+1}\) and \(Z_jZ_{j+1}=i\gamma_{2j+1}\gamma_{2j+2}\), and the Hamiltonian is quadratic,

\[H=\frac i4\sum_{m,n=0}^{2N-1}A_{mn}\gamma_m\gamma_n,\qquad A_{2j,2j+1}=-2h,\quad A_{2j+1,2j+2}=-2J,\quad A=-A^{\rm T}, \qquad G_{mn}=\langle i\gamma_m\gamma_n\rangle=i\,\mathrm{sgn}(iA)_{mn}\ (m\neq n), \tag{15}\]

where \(\mathrm{sgn}(iA)\) is the Hermitian matrix \(iA\) with every eigenvalue replaced by its sign, and \(E_0=\frac14\sum_{mn}A_{mn}G_{mn}\). The form of \(G\) follows in three steps. The ground state of a quadratic Hamiltonian is a fermionic Gaussian state, fixed by its matrix \(G\), which is real and antisymmetric; for a pure Gaussian state \(G^2=-\mathbb 1\) (quoted here without proof). Then \(S=-iG\) is Hermitian with \(S^2=\mathbb 1\), so its eigenvalues are \(\pm1\). The energy is \(E=\frac14\sum_{mn}A_{mn}G_{mn}=-\frac14\mathrm{tr}\big[(iA)S\big]\), and over Hermitian \(S\) with eigenvalues \(\pm1\) the trace is largest for \(S=\mathrm{sgn}(iA)\), which gives every eigenvector of \(iA\) the sign of its eigenvalue, that is, fills every negative mode. Hence \(G=i\,\mathrm{sgn}(iA)\) and \(E_0=-\frac14\sum_k\vert\varepsilon_k\vert\), with \(\varepsilon_k\) the eigenvalues of \(iA\). The local statistics are single entries, \(\langle X_j\rangle=G_{2j,2j+1}\) and \(\langle Z_jZ_{j+1}\rangle=G_{2j+1,2j+2}\). The two long operators are products of \(L=N/2\) such pairs. By Wick’s theorem, in a fermionic Gaussian state the expectation value of a product of Majorana operators is the Pfaffian of the matrix of their two-point correlations; because \(A\) couples only even to odd Majoranas, the even–even and odd–odd correlations vanish and the Pfaffians become determinants,

\[\langle Z_{j_1}Z_{j_2}\rangle=\big\vert\det\big[G_{2k+1,\,2l+2}\big]_{k,l=j_1}^{j_2-1}\big\vert,\qquad \Big\langle\prod_{k=j_1}^{j_2-1}X_k\Big\rangle=\big\vert\det\big[G_{2k,\,2l+1}\big]_{k,l=j_1}^{j_2-1}\big\vert, \tag{16}\]

at a cost of \(O(N^3)\) for any \(N\) (the absolute value fixes the sign convention; both quantities are non-negative in the ground state, because the string flips bits in the \(Z\) basis, \(Z_{j_1}Z_{j_2}\) flips two bits in the \(X\) basis, and in both bases the ground state has non-negative amplitudes, Section 3.1). For the infinite chain, the derivatives of the exact energy per site \(e=-\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\sqrt{J^2+h^2+2hJ\cos q}\,{\rm d}q\) with respect to \(h\) and \(J\) give the two local expectation values by the Hellmann–Feynman theorem: the derivative of the ground-state energy with respect to a parameter of \(H\) equals the expectation value of the derivative of \(H\) (\(\partial e/\partial h=-\langle X_j\rangle\), \(\partial e/\partial J=-\langle Z_jZ_{j+1}\rangle\)):

\[\langle X_j\rangle_\infty=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\frac{h+J\cos q}{\sqrt{J^2+h^2+2hJ\cos q}}\,{\rm d}q,\qquad \langle Z_jZ_{j+1}\rangle_\infty=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\frac{J+h\cos q}{\sqrt{J^2+h^2+2hJ\cos q}}\,{\rm d}q. \tag{17}\]

def majorana_correlations(N, h, J=1.0):
    """Ground-state Majorana correlation matrix G[m, n] = <i gamma_m gamma_n> (m != n) of the open chain, Eq. (15).

    MATH            H = (i/4) sum_{mn} A_mn gamma_m gamma_n,  A[2j, 2j+1] = -2h,  A[2j+1, 2j+2] = -2J,  A = -A^T;
                    ground state: G = i sgn(iA) (off-diagonal part), energy E_0 = (1/4) sum_{mn} A_mn G_mn
    COST            one Hermitian eigendecomposition of size 2N: O(N^3)
    RETURNS         G (2N, 2N), E_0
    """
    A = np.zeros((2 * N, 2 * N))
    A[2 * np.arange(N), 2 * np.arange(N) + 1] = -2 * h
    A[2 * np.arange(N - 1) + 1, 2 * np.arange(N - 1) + 2] = -2 * J
    A = A - A.T
    w, V = np.linalg.eigh(1j * A)
    G = np.real(1j * (V * np.sign(w)) @ V.conj().T)
    return G, 0.25 * np.sum(A * G)

def pair_crossing(h, f_small, f_large):

    return h[k - 1] - d[k - 1] * (h[k] - h[k - 1]) / (d[k] - d[k - 1])

7. Limits of the methods

  • The prior decides everything in Part A. One basis determines the state only if the state is pure and the phases of its amplitudes are known in advance, here non-negative. Section 4.9 showed that a sign pattern or complete dephasing leaves the \(Z\) snapshots unchanged and destroys the fidelity, and that only measurements in another basis reveal the failure. For a laboratory state that is neither exactly pure nor exactly sign-free, the reconstruction is the closest positive pure state in the likelihood sense, and Eq. (8) cannot be evaluated without knowing the true state.
  • Fidelities need the truth. The divergences and fidelities of Section 4 were computed with the exact \(\ln p\) of the DMRG state. An experiment has only the snapshots; the held-out NLL measures \(S(p)+D_{\rm KL}\), and \(S(p)\) is unknown. The certified fidelity bound of [3] needs data in all local Pauli bases.
  • Bias of the reconstruction. The energy of the reconstruction is precise but biased upwards (Section 4.7). The bias is not bounded by the fidelity in a useful way when the gap is small, and error bars from the model’s own samples do not cover it.
  • One dimension. The MPS suits chains. For two-dimensional ground states the bond dimension must grow exponentially with the width of the system.
  • A classifier crossing is not a pseudo-critical point. The field where a classifier output crosses a fixed level tracks the field where the statistic it follows reaches the level set by the training windows, whose limit is \(0.973\) for the order parameter, tracked up to \(N=64\) by the \(Z\)-basis classifier here, and \(1.020\) for the disorder string. Which statistic a classifier follows is not controlled (the \(X\)-basis classifier crossed between a local statistic and the string). Crossings of two system sizes of a quantity with a scaling form, such as the Binder cumulant, converge to \(h_c\); those of our \(Z\)-basis classifier agree with \(h_c\) within their errors.
  • Labels need prior knowledge. The supervised classifier needs two fields known to lie in different phases. The confusion scheme of Exercise 6 trains with trial labels instead and needs no prior knowledge of where the phases lie.

8. Key takeaways

  • Snapshots as data. Measurement records of a many-body state are bit strings drawn from the Born distribution of the measured basis; an MPS state can be sampled exactly in any product basis by a gate on its physical legs, and a DMRG ground state with a parity projection (Eq. (6)) supplies them for chains of 64 spins.
  • Tomography with a prior. For a pure state with non-negative amplitudes, \(\psi=\sqrt p\) (Eq. (5)), which ground states of stoquastic Hamiltonians satisfy. An MPS Born machine trained on \(20000\) \(Z\) snapshots reconstructed the critical 32-spin Ising ground state with \(F=0.9931\pm0.0012\), with the bond dimension and the number of loops chosen on a validation set, and \(F\geq e^{-D_{\rm KL}}\) (Eq. (9)).
  • Observables from one basis. Local estimators (Eqs. (10)–(11)) give off-diagonal observables of a positive wave function from its \(Z\) snapshots: the transverse magnetisation within two standard errors of DMRG, the energy with a small, always positive bias of up to \(0.0041\) per site.
  • Where one basis fails. A sign structure or dephasing is invisible in the \(Z\) basis; the \(X\) basis detects both and, for single-site signs, repairs the reconstruction. General MPS tomography measures blocks of \(k\) neighbouring qubits in all \(3^k\) Pauli settings and certifies its result.
  • Classification is property learning. An MPS classifier trained far from the transition locates it in either basis. In the \(Z\) basis its fixed-level crossings (\(0.878\), \(0.921\), \(0.948\) for \(N=16\), \(32\), \(64\)) track the exact crossings of the order parameter at the same \(N\), whose limit is \(0.973\), while the crossings of its outputs for two sizes, like the Binder cumulants, give \(h_c=1\) within their errors.
  • Exact state vectors as the reference. At \(N=16\) the DMRG states match exact Lanczos ground states to an infidelity of at most \(5.4\times10^{-10}\), and snapshots, reconstructions and classifier crossings from the exact vectors agree with their MPS counterparts within statistics. One state vector needs \(2^N\times16\) bytes, \(68.7\) GB at \(N=32\), while the stored MPS of \(N=64\) spins takes \(2.10\) MB.
  • Duality in data. The order parameter in \(Z\) snapshots and the disorder string in \(X\) snapshots are dual under the Kramers–Wannier duality (Eq. (13)), and their curves are related approximately by \(h\leftrightarrow J^2/h\).

9. Exercises

  1. ★ Zero variance. Show from Eq. (11) that \(E_{\rm loc}(\mathbf s)=E\) for every \(\mathbf s\) if \(\psi_{\rm rec}\) is an eigenstate with eigenvalue \(E\). Compute the standard deviation of \(E_{\rm loc}\) for the reconstructions of Section 4.6 and plot it against \(1-F\).
  2. ★ The bound (9). When is \(F=e^{-D_{\rm KL}}\)? Find two distributions on two bits for which the bound is far from tight, and check Eq. (9) on the twelve runs of Section 4.6.
  3. ★ Dephased snapshots. Generate \(X\) snapshots of a partially dephased state, in which each qubit is dephased with probability \(q\) (on each snapshot, replace the \(X\) outcome of that qubit by a random bit with probability \(q\)). How does the mean \(\langle X_j\rangle\) depend on \(q\), and from how many snapshots can \(q=0.05\) be detected at \(h=1\)?
  4. ★★ Reconstruction from \(X\) snapshots (extend the code). The ground state is also positive in the \(X\) basis, on the even strings (Section 3.1). Train the Born machine on \(X\) snapshots at \(h=1\) and \(h=0.5\), and compare the fidelity with that of Section 4.7. Which basis is the better one in each phase, and why?
  5. ★★ Signs that one basis does not fix (physics). Apply a controlled-\(Z\) gate to every bond of the ground state. Show that the \(Z\) snapshots do not change, that \(\langle X_j\rangle\) of the new state equals \(\langle Z_{j-1}X_jZ_{j+1}\rangle\) of the old one, and compute it with the MPS. Which three-site measurement settings detect the signs, and how does this relate to the blocks of \(k=3\) qubits of Section 4.10?
  6. ★★ Learning by confusion. The confusion scheme was introduced in [15]: label the snapshots of all fields below a trial field \(h'\) as one phase and the rest as the other, train the classifier and record its accuracy on fresh snapshots, for \(h'\) on the grid. Plot the accuracy against \(h'\) for \(N=32\) in the \(Z\) basis; where is the peak of the W-shaped curve?
  7. ★★ Training windows. Move the training windows to \(\{0.5,0.7\}\) and \(\{1.3,1.5\}\) (fields of the grid) and repeat Section 5 for both bases. Compute the new exact limit of the order-parameter crossing from \(m^2(h)\), and check whether the \(Z\)-basis crossings follow the exact crossings at each \(N\).
  8. ★★★ Two bases, one complex model (extend the code). Write the negative log-likelihood of a complex MPS for \(Z\) and \(X\) snapshots together, with the \(X\) term computed from the Hadamard-rotated tensors, and train it on snapshots of the signed state \(\psi_\sigma\) by automatic differentiation. Compare the fidelity with that of the sign-corrected reconstruction of Section 4.9.
